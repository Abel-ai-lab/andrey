# Functional causal models

## TL;DR

- **Direction from nonlinear effects:** CAM and RESIT orient every edge when effects are curves,
  where PC and GES leave some edges undirected and LiNGAM methods need straight lines.
- **As accurate as the authors' code, and faster:** CAM makes no more errors than CAM 1.0 in any of
  16 settings and is 6.5 to 14.7 times faster; RESIT makes no more errors than its authors' code in
  any of 12 and is 3.2 to 4.6 times faster.
- **Hidden variables:** the experimental CAM-UV marks the pairs a hidden variable joins instead of
  guessing their direction.
- **Also in 0.13.0:** IAMB and MMPC are supported, DYNOTEARS learns from time series
  (experimental), and results no longer depend on the row order.

---

Most causal discovery methods can tell which variables are connected, but not always which way.
In 0.13.0, Andrey reads the direction from the shape of an effect, even when the effect is a curve.

## Structural causal models

A structural causal model writes each variable as a function of its direct causes, its parents,
and a noise term of its own:

$$x_j = f_j(\mathrm{pa}_j, e_j), \qquad j = 1, \dots, d,$$

with the noise terms independent of each other. The graph draws an arrow from each parent to its
child, and the data are draws of all the $x_j$.

When nothing restricts the functions $f_j$ or the noise, the data reveal only which variables are
independent given which others. Graphs that differ in the direction of some edges imply the same
independences, so PC and GES, which read independences, return those edges undirected.

## Functional causal models

A functional causal model restricts the form of each $f_j$ and its noise, and under the
restriction only one direction fits the data. Take $y = f(x) + e$, with $e$ independent of $x$.
Regress $y$ on $x$, and the residual is $e$, independent of $x$. Regress $x$ on $y$, and when $f$
is nonlinear, the residual still depends on $y$. Only the true direction leaves independent
residuals
([Hoyer et al., 2008](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3548-nonlinear-causal-discovery-with-additive-noise-models)).

Each functional causal model in Andrey assumes its own form of $f_j$:

| Method | Model of each variable | The direction comes from | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| DirectLiNGAM, ICA-LiNGAM | $x_j = \sum_{k \in \mathrm{pa}_j} b_{jk}\, x_k + e_j$, with $e_j$ non-Gaussian | the shape of the noise | DAG |
| ANM | $y = f(x) + e$ | a nonlinear $f$ | the direction of one pair |
| PNL | $y = g(f(x) + e)$, with $g$ invertible | a nonlinear $f$ and $g$ | the direction of one pair |
| CAM, new | $x_j = \sum_{k \in \mathrm{pa}_j} f_{jk}(x_k) + e_j$ | a nonlinear $f_{jk}$ | DAG and causal order |
| RESIT, new | $x_j = f_j(\mathrm{pa}_j) + e_j$, with $e_j$ of any distribution | a nonlinear $f_j$ | DAG and causal order |
| CAM-UV, new, experimental | CAM's model, with some variables hidden | a nonlinear $f_{jk}$ | PAG, `o-o` where a hidden variable joins a pair |

The LiNGAM methods, CAM, and RESIT now form one family under the name "Functional causal models";
ANM and PNL, which take one pair at a time, stay under "Pairwise".

**CAM** ([Bühlmann, Peters, and Ernest, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOS1260)) makes each
effect a sum of one smooth function per parent and scores a graph by the Gaussian likelihood of
its residuals. It picks each variable's possible parents, adds the edges that most reduce the
residual variance, and prunes the smooth terms that are not significant. A score is cheap to
compute, so CAM reaches 100 variables.

**RESIT**
([Peters, Mooij, Janzing, and Schölkopf, 2014](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/peters14a.html))
assumes nothing about the noise distribution. It builds the causal order from the last variable
up, each time taking the variable whose residual a kernel test finds most independent of all the
remaining variables, then drops each parent whose removal keeps the residual independent. Andrey
fits each $f_j$ as a sum of smooth terms, as CAM does. Each test reads all the remaining variables
at once, so RESIT suits a handful of them.

**CAM-UV** ([Maeda and Shimizu, 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v161/maeda21a.html)) keeps
CAM's model and allows hidden variables. Where a hidden common cause or a hidden step on a causal
path joins two variables, no regression can tell their relation, and it marks the pair `o-o`
instead of guessing.

## Checked against the authors' code

We compared each method with the code of its authors on simulated data from the model it assumes.
CAM makes no more errors than CAM 1.0 in any of 16 settings from 10 to 100 variables, fewer in 2,
and returns the same graph on 278 of 320 datasets, 6.5 to 14.7 times faster on one core. RESIT
makes no more errors than the authors' R code in any of 12 settings from 5 to 20 variables, one of
them after a rerun on fresh data, 3.2 to 4.6 times faster; against lingam's RESIT, it makes fewer
errors in 11 and is 2.1 to 2.9 times faster. CAM-UV is on par with lingam's and causal-learn's
versions, 2.7 to 3.7 and 7.5 to 16 times faster. The
[release card](../docs/benchmarks/0.13.0.md) has every comparison.

## Also in 0.13.0

**IAMB and MMPC are supported.** Every Markov-blanket method in Andrey, and the hybrids MMHC and
RSMAX2 built on MMPC, are now supported. Against bnlearn on linear-Gaussian data, IAMB's CPDAG is
4.6 times faster in the median and MMPC's skeleton 7.6 times. Their chi-square and G-square tests
are 1.6 to 2.9 times faster than in 0.12.0, with the same results.

**DYNOTEARS for time series.** DYNOTEARS
([Pamfil et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/pamfil20a.html)) learns the effects
within a time step and from earlier steps, with their weights, from one series. It is experimental,
and a later release card will compare it with the reference implementation.

**Location-scale noise.** `sample_scm(functional="location_scale")` draws data whose noise scale
depends on the parents, outside the model of CAM and RESIT. We use it to see how they fail there.

**Order-free tie breaking.** When two choices score the same, a search breaks the tie by a rank of
the columns. That rank no longer depends on the row order, a column's units, or its sign. Hill
climbing, Tabu search, BOSS, GRaSP, FCI, and a few others can return another graph where a tie
decided it.

DirectLiNGAM, ICA-LiNGAM, and multi-group DirectLiNGAM report the family `"functional"` in
`andrey list`, where they reported `"lingam"`. The [changelog](../docs/changelog.md) lists every
change.

## Limits

**The model must fit.** CAM and RESIT assume nonlinear effects with additive noise and no hidden
common causes. With linear effects and Gaussian noise, the data identify only the equivalence
class; noise whose scale depends on the parents can reverse edges.

**RESIT is for small graphs.** Its accuracy claim holds up to 10 variables. At 20, on Gaussian
noise, no implementation we measured clearly beats the empty graph.

**Many hidden variables defeat CAM-UV.** Where hidden variables carry most of the edges, neither
CAM-UV nor the reference implementations make fewer errors than the empty graph, and all of them
mark few of the pairs a hidden variable joins.

**Discrete networks are slower.** On discrete networks bnlearn is faster than IAMB and MMPC in most
settings, and on hepar2, with 70 variables, in every one.

# Functional causal models

## TL;DR

- **CAM:** nonlinear effects with additive noise, every edge oriented. No more errors than CAM 1.0,
  its authors' R package, in any of 16 settings, and 6.5 to 14.7 times faster.
- **RESIT:** the same model with noise of any distribution. No more errors than the authors' code
  in any of 12 settings, fewer than lingam's in 11; 3.2 to 4.6 times faster than the authors' code
  and 2.1 to 2.9 times faster than lingam.
- **Two experimental methods:** CAM-UV marks the pairs a hidden variable joins, and DYNOTEARS
  learns same-step and lagged effects from a time series.
- **IAMB and MMPC supported:** every Markov-blanket method in Andrey, and the hybrids built on
  MMPC, are supported, and their tests on categorical data are faster, with the same results.
- **Order-free ties:** a result no longer depends on the row order, a column's units, or its sign.

---

In 0.13.0, Andrey learns which way an effect runs when the effect is not a straight line.

A functional causal model writes each variable as a function of its causes plus independent noise.
The functions and the noise leave a trace that a test of conditional independence cannot see: fit
an effect in the right direction, and what is left over is independent of the cause; fit it in the
wrong one, and it is not. That trace orients edges that PC and GES must leave undirected.

LiNGAM methods read this trace in linear models with non-Gaussian noise. CAM and RESIT read it in
nonlinear ones, and with them DirectLiNGAM, ICA-LiNGAM, and multi-group DirectLiNGAM now form one
family, "Functional causal models".

## Causal additive models

CAM ([Bühlmann, Peters, and Ernest, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOS1260)) assumes each
effect is a sum of smooth functions of its parents plus independent noise. Boosting first picks each
variable's possible parents, a greedy search then adds the edges that most reduce the residual
variance, and tests of each smooth term prune the edges that do not hold. It returns a DAG and a
causal order.

Against CAM 1.0 on simulated data from 10 to 100 variables, CAM makes no more errors in any of the
16 settings of its model, fewer in 2, and returns the same graph on 278 of 320 datasets. It is 6.5
to 14.7 times faster on one core.

## Regression with independence tests

RESIT ([Peters, Mooij, Janzing, and Schölkopf, 2014](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/peters14a.html))
fits the same kind of model with noise of any distribution. It finds the causal order from the
last variable up: the next one is the variable whose residual a kernel independence test finds most
independent of the rest. Then it drops each parent whose removal leaves the residual independent.

In the 12 settings of its model, from 5 to 20 variables, RESIT makes no more errors than the
authors' R code, one setting after a rerun on fresh data, and fewer than lingam's RESIT in 11. Its
accuracy claim holds up to 10 variables; at 20, no implementation clearly beats the empty graph. It
is 3.2 to 4.6 times faster than the authors' code and 2.1 to 2.9 times faster than lingam. Each test
reads all the remaining variables at once, so RESIT suits a handful of them; use CAM beyond ten.

## Hidden variables with CAM-UV

CAM-UV ([Maeda and Shimizu, 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v161/maeda21a.html)) allows
hidden variables. Where a hidden common cause or a hidden step on a causal path joins two
variables, no regression can tell their relation, and CAM-UV marks the pair `o-o` instead of
guessing. Its graphs are on par with the two reference implementations, lingam's and
causal-learn's, and it is 2.7 to 3.7 times faster than lingam's and 7.5 to 16 times faster than
causal-learn's. It is experimental: like the references, it marks few of the pairs a hidden
variable joins.

## DYNOTEARS for time series

DYNOTEARS ([Pamfil et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/pamfil20a.html)) learns a
linear structural vector autoregression from one series: the effects within a time step and from
earlier steps, with their weights. It fits by least squares with a sparsity penalty, under the
NOTEARS acyclicity constraint on the same-step effects. It is experimental, and a later release
card will compare it with the reference implementation.

## Location-scale noise

`sample_scm(functional="location_scale")` draws data whose noise scale depends on the parents. That
breaks the additive-noise model, and we use it to see how CAM and RESIT fail outside their claim.
The release card reports those settings without judging them.

## Supported Markov-blanket methods

IAMB and MMPC move from experimental to supported, so every Markov-blanket method in Andrey is
supported: IAMB, Inter-IAMB, and MMPC, and the hybrids MMHC and RSMAX2 built on MMPC. Against
bnlearn on linear-Gaussian data, IAMB's CPDAG is 4.6 times faster in the median, and MMPC's
skeleton 7.6 times.

We also measured the five on five discrete networks, from asia to hepar2. There their graphs make
fewer errors than bnlearn's in most settings, but they are slower. The chi-square and G-square
tests now count the tables of all the candidates of a conditioning set together, which makes IAMB,
Inter-IAMB, and MMPC 1.6 to 2.9 times faster with the same graphs and p-values, and the G-square
test another 1.4 to 1.6 times. bnlearn is still faster on every network but the smallest.

## Order-free tie breaking

When two choices score the same, a search breaks the tie by a rank of the columns. That rank now
comes from each column's relations to the others, so the result no longer changes when the rows are
shuffled, a column's units change, or its sign flips. Reordering the columns relabels the result.
The graphs of hill climbing, Tabu search, BOSS, GRaSP, FCI, and a few others can change where a tie
decided them; on fresh data, their errors match those of a random ranking.

## Other changes

DirectLiNGAM, ICA-LiNGAM, and multi-group DirectLiNGAM report the family `"functional"` in
`andrey list`, where they reported `"lingam"`. `load_dataset` adds the child and insurance
networks. RCD, GIN, and VARMA-LiNGAM no longer change
their graph when a large constant is added to every column. The [changelog](../docs/changelog.md)
lists every change.

## Limits

**The model must fit.** CAM and RESIT assume nonlinear effects with additive noise and no hidden
common causes. With linear effects and Gaussian noise, the data identify only the equivalence
class; noise whose scale depends on the parents can reverse edges.

**RESIT is for small graphs.** Its accuracy claim holds up to 10 variables. At 20, on Gaussian
noise, no implementation we measured clearly beats the empty graph.

**Many hidden variables defeat CAM-UV.** Where hidden variables carry most of the edges, neither
CAM-UV nor the reference implementations make fewer errors than the empty graph.

**Discrete networks are slower.** On the discrete networks bnlearn is faster than Andrey in most
settings, and on hepar2, with 70 variables, in every one.

# Andrey supports mixed and categorical data

Your tables may contain more than numbers. A customer record holds a plan and a region alongside
hours used and the amount billed. A patient record holds a diagnosis alongside blood pressure. With
Andrey 0.6.0, you can learn causal graphs from categorical data and from tables that mix categorical
and continuous columns.

## TL;DR

- GES on categorical data: more than 50x faster than causal-learn at every size, with the same
  graph on every dataset.
- Hill climbing on categorical data: more than 20x faster than pgmpy at every size.
- PC on mixed data: more than 10x faster than Tetrad at 10 and 20 variables.

We explain how Andrey reads each kind of column, run an example, and report benchmark results.

## Tests and scores

A constraint-based method tests whether two variables are independent given others. A score-based
method compares graphs by how well they explain the data. Each needs a test or score that fits your
data. In Andrey 0.6.0 you can use both with categorical and mixed data.

| Data | Tests | Scores |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical | `"chisq"` (chi-square), `"gsq"` (G-square) | `"local_score_BDeu"`, `"local_score_BIC_categorical"` (multinomial BIC) |
| Mixed | `"degen_gauss"` (degenerate Gaussian), `"cond_gauss"` (conditional Gaussian) | `"local_score_degen_gauss"`, `"local_score_cond_gauss"` |

PC, FCI, GFCI, and CD-NOD take every test. GES, GFCI, hill climbing, and exact search take every
score; Tabu search takes the categorical scores, and BOSS and GRaSP the mixed ones. GFCI, CD-NOD,
and exact search are experimental.

The two mixed-data forms make different assumptions:

- **Degenerate Gaussian** replaces a categorical column of `k` categories with `k - 1` indicator
  columns and treats every column as Gaussian. It suits effects that add up across categories. It is
  the faster of the two, though only slightly in PC.
- **Conditional Gaussian** fits a Gaussian with its own mean and covariance to the continuous
  variables within each combination of categories. A category can change the spread as well as the
  average. It needs several rows in each combination.

You do not need to encode a DataFrame's text, `bool`, or `category` columns; Andrey reads them as
categorical. With a mixed-data test or score, Andrey also reads a column with at most 10 distinct
whole numbers as categorical and issues a warning. To be sure, name the categorical columns with
`categorical=[...]`, or change the limit with `max_categories`.

## Benchmarks

We compare methods on simulated categorical and conditional linear Gaussian networks with 10, 20,
and 40 variables and 2,000 rows. We use 5 categorical datasets and 10 mixed datasets at each size.
We compare each method with packages that implement it: causal-learn and pgmpy in Python, bnlearn in
R, and Tetrad in Java.

| Method | Data | Compared with | Andrey's speedup | Graph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GES, BDeu score | Categorical | causal-learn | more than 50x at every size | the same as causal-learn's on every dataset |
| Hill climbing, BDeu and BIC scores | Categorical | pgmpy | more than 20x at every size | the same scores; the graphs differ on 28 of 30 datasets, with fewer errors on 9 and more on 17; on those 17 the two searches part at a tie between two equal moves, which they break differently |
| PC, conditional Gaussian test | Mixed | Tetrad | more than 10x at 10 and 20 variables | fewer errors on 12 of the 18 datasets Tetrad finished, more on 4 |
| GES, conditional Gaussian score | Mixed | Tetrad (FGES) | more than 9x at every size | the same on 11 of 30 datasets; of the other 19, fewer errors on 11 and more on 7 |
| PC, chi-square and G-square tests | Categorical | bnlearn | more than 3x at 20 and 40 variables | fewer errors on 24 of 30 datasets, more on 2 |
| PC, chi-square and G-square tests | Categorical | causal-learn | more than 2x at 20 and 40 variables | the same as causal-learn's on every dataset |

Each speedup holds at every size it names. At 10 variables, PC on categorical data takes under 0.1 s
in every package, too fast to time fairly. Its speedups therefore start at 20. Tetrad's PC with the
conditional Gaussian test stopped with an error on 12 of its 30 fits. The table counts only the
datasets both packages finished.

You can find every measurement in the [0.6.0 release card](../docs/benchmarks/0.6.0.md), including
comparisons with bnlearn's hill climbing and the degenerate Gaussian forms. It also shows where
Andrey is not faster and gives the machine, settings, and known limits.

## More features

- **Simulated networks.** `andrey.data.sample_discrete` and `sample_mixed` sample a categorical or a
  conditional linear Gaussian network along with its true graph.
- **Published networks as categorical data.** `load_dataset("alarm", discrete=True)` samples ASIA,
  ALARM, HEPAR2, or ANDES from the network's own probability tables.
- **Hill climbing respects the model.** With a mixed-data score, `hc` never adds an edge from a
  continuous variable to a categorical one. A conditional linear Gaussian network cannot express
  that edge.

The categorical tests and scores build on work by Rui Zhu
([#94](https://github.com/Abel-ai-lab/andrey/pull/94)). Thank you, Rui.

For details, read the [changelog](../docs/changelog.md).

## Try it

```{code-block} shell
:class: window

pip install -U andrey-core
```

Pass a DataFrame with text or category columns to `andrey.pc(data, indep_test="cond_gauss")` or
`andrey.ges(data, score_func="local_score_cond_gauss")`, or use `"chisq"` and `"local_score_BDeu"`
when every column is categorical.

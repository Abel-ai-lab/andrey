# Andrey supports mixed and categorical data

Most tables are not all numbers. A customer record holds a plan and a region next to the hours
used and the amount billed; a patient record holds a diagnosis next to a blood pressure. As of
0.6.0, Andrey learns causal graphs from categorical data and from tables that mix categorical and
continuous columns.

## TL;DR

- GES on categorical data: more than 50x faster than causal-learn at every size, with the same
  graph on every dataset.
- Hill climbing on categorical data: more than 20x faster than pgmpy at every size.
- PC on mixed data: more than 10x faster than Tetrad at 10 and 20 variables.

We explain how Andrey reads each kind of column, run an example, and report benchmark results.

## A test or a score for each kind of column

A constraint-based method asks whether two variables are independent given others, so it needs a
test that fits the data. A score-based method compares graphs by how well they explain the data,
so it needs a score that fits. Andrey 0.6.0 adds both for two new kinds of data.

| Data | Tests | Scores |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical | `"chisq"` (chi-square), `"gsq"` (G-square) | `"local_score_BDeu"`, `"local_score_BIC_categorical"` (multinomial BIC) |
| Mixed | `"degen_gauss"` (degenerate Gaussian), `"cond_gauss"` (conditional Gaussian) | `"local_score_degen_gauss"`, `"local_score_cond_gauss"` |

PC, FCI, GFCI, and CD-NOD take every test. GES, GFCI, hill climbing, and exact search take every
score; Tabu search takes the categorical scores, and BOSS and GRaSP the mixed ones. GFCI, CD-NOD,
and exact search are experimental.

The two mixed-data forms differ in what they assume:

- **Degenerate Gaussian** replaces a categorical column of `k` categories by `k - 1` indicator
  columns and treats every column as Gaussian. It suits effects that add up across categories,
  and it is the faster of the two, though only slightly in PC.
- **Conditional Gaussian** fits the continuous variables within each combination of categories
  with a Gaussian of its own mean and covariance, so a category can change a spread as well as an
  average. It needs several rows in each combination.

A DataFrame's text, `bool`, and `category` columns are categorical, so they need no coding. With a
mixed-data test or score, a column of at most 10 distinct whole numbers is read as categorical
too, with a warning; name the categorical columns with `categorical=[...]` to be sure, or change
the limit with `max_categories`.

## Benchmark results

We simulated categorical networks and conditional linear Gaussian networks with 10, 20, and 40
variables and 2,000 rows: 5 datasets at each size of categorical data and 10 of mixed data. We
compared each method with packages that implement it: causal-learn and pgmpy in Python, bnlearn
in R, and Tetrad in Java.

| Method | Data | Compared with | Andrey's speedup | Graph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GES, BDeu score | Categorical | causal-learn | more than 50x at every size | the same as causal-learn's on every dataset |
| Hill climbing, BDeu and BIC scores | Categorical | pgmpy | more than 20x at every size | the same score; the two break ties between equal moves differently, so the graphs differ on 28 of 30 datasets: fewer errors on 9, more on 17 |
| PC, conditional Gaussian test | Mixed | Tetrad | more than 10x at 10 and 20 variables | fewer errors on 12 of the 18 datasets Tetrad finished, more on 4 |
| GES, conditional Gaussian score | Mixed | Tetrad (FGES) | more than 9x at every size | the same on 11 of 30 datasets; of the other 19, fewer errors on 11 and more on 7 |
| PC, chi-square and G-square tests | Categorical | bnlearn | more than 3x at 20 and 40 variables | fewer errors on 24 of 30 datasets, more on 2 |
| PC, chi-square and G-square tests | Categorical | causal-learn | more than 2x at 20 and 40 variables | the same as causal-learn's on every dataset |

Each speedup holds at every size it names. At 10 variables, PC on categorical data took under
0.1 s in every package, too fast to time fairly, so its speedups start at 20. Tetrad's PC with the
conditional Gaussian test stopped with an error on 12 of its 30 fits; the table counts only the
datasets both packages finished.

The [0.6.0 release card](../docs/benchmarks/0.6.0.md) reports every measurement, including the
comparisons with bnlearn's hill climbing and the degenerate Gaussian forms, and where Andrey is
not faster. It also gives the machine, settings, and known limits.

## Other highlights

- **Simulated networks.** `andrey.data.sample_discrete` and `sample_mixed` sample a categorical
  or a conditional linear Gaussian network along with its true graph.
- **Published networks as categorical data.** `load_dataset("alarm", discrete=True)` samples
  ASIA, ALARM, HEPAR2, or ANDES from the network's own probability tables.
- **Hill climbing respects the model.** With a mixed-data score, `hc` never adds an edge from a
  continuous variable to a categorical one, which a conditional linear Gaussian network cannot
  express.

The categorical tests and scores build on work by Rui Zhu
([#94](https://github.com/Abel-ai-lab/andrey/pull/94)). Thank you, Rui.

## Try it

```{code-block} shell
:class: window

pip install -U andrey-core
```

Pass a DataFrame with text or category columns to `andrey.pc(data, indep_test="cond_gauss")` or
`andrey.ges(data, score_func="local_score_cond_gauss")`, or use `"chisq"` and
`"local_score_BDeu"` when every column is categorical.

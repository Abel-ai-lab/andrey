::::{div} release-opening-text

We compare PC, GES, and hill climbing on categorical and mixed data with the packages that implement
them: causal-learn and pgmpy in Python, bnlearn in R, and Tetrad in Java. On categorical data, GES
with the BDeu score runs more than 50x faster than causal-learn at every size and returns the same
graph on every dataset. Hill climbing runs more than 20x faster than pgmpy at every size. On mixed
data, PC with the conditional Gaussian test runs more than 10x faster than Tetrad at 10 and 20
variables, and GES with the conditional Gaussian score more than 9x faster than Tetrad's FGES at
every size. We support these tests and scores in 0.6.0.

Speed is not accuracy. Andrey and pgmpy maximize the same hill climbing score, yet pgmpy's graphs
have fewer errors on more datasets (see [Limits](#limits)). You can find
speedups and accuracy in the [summary](#summary), and measurement and scoring definitions in
[Method](#method). These results apply to the measured workloads. Speed and accuracy depend on the
data, settings, and hardware.

## Method

**Data.** Categorical data come from `discrete_er`: a discrete Bayesian network on an Erdos-Renyi
DAG with mean degree 2 (`andrey.data.sample_discrete`). Every variable has 2 or 3 categories, and
each row of its conditional probability table is drawn from a flat Dirichlet. Mixed data come from
`mixed_er`: a conditional linear Gaussian network on the same kind of DAG
(`andrey.data.sample_mixed`). The first half of the variables in causal order are categorical, and a
categorical variable has only categorical parents. Each continuous variable is a linear function of
its continuous parents, with a shift for each category of its categorical parents and Gaussian
noise. `d` is the number of variables and `n` the number of rows, 2,000 throughout. There are 5
datasets per size of categorical data, one per seed, and 10 of mixed data: 5 seeds with 2 to 3
categories per categorical variable and 5 with 3 to 5.

**Columns.** Every package reads a column as categorical when it holds at most 10 distinct whole
numbers: Andrey with `max_categories=10`, Tetrad with `--numCategories 10`, and bnlearn by turning
such columns into factors. On these datasets, the rule identifies exactly the generated categorical
columns.

**Accuracy.** Errors are the SHD between the estimate's CPDAG and the true DAG's CPDAG: one for each
pair of variables whose edge is missing, extra, or oriented differently. We compare DAG estimates,
which bnlearn's and pgmpy's hill climbing return, through their CPDAGs. The empty graph's errors are
the number of true edges. PC against bnlearn on mixed data counts skeleton errors only (see
[Mixed PC](#mixed-pc)). Errors are medians over a size's datasets. "Same graph" means the two
packages' CPDAGs are equal.

**Timing.** Times are seconds per fit, the median over a size's datasets. For each dataset, we
divide the other package's time by Andrey's, then report the median of these paired ratios. This
need not equal the ratio of the displayed median times. We show no speedup for a size at which both
packages' median times are under 0.1 s, and leave it out of the summary. Tetrad's time covers the
search inside the Java virtual machine, excluding startup and data reading. Andrey's time excludes
Python's startup and import. Times and ratios have three significant figures.

**Hardware.** Every fit ran on an Intel Core i7-7820X with 4 cores per fit.

## Categorical data

### Categorical PC

PC tests whether two variables are independent given others, removes the edges its tests cannot
keep, and orients what the remaining colliders allow. Andrey's chi-square (`"chisq"`) and G-square
(`"gsq"`) tests compare the counts of two variables with independence within each configuration of
the conditioning set. Both packages test at `alpha` 0.05 with no limit on the conditioning set.
bnlearn's PC runs its `-adf` tests (`x2-adf`, `mi-adf`). These exclude empty rows and columns from a
table's degrees of freedom, as Andrey's tests do.

### Categorical hill climbing

until none improves the score, with no parent limit and no restarts. With the BDeu score, every
package uses an equivalent sample size of 1 and a structure prior of one expected parent per
variable. pgmpy has no such prior, so the harness adds Andrey's through pgmpy's own hook. The BIC
score penalizes `log(n) / 2` per parameter in every package.

## Mixed data

### Mixed PC

The conditional Gaussian test (`"cond_gauss"`) fits the continuous variables within each combination
of categories with a Gaussian of its own mean and covariance. The degenerate Gaussian test
(`"degen_gauss"`) replaces each categorical variable of `k` categories with `k - 1` indicator
columns and treats every column as Gaussian. Both compare the likelihoods of the models with and
without the dependence. Andrey and Tetrad run order-independent PC at `alpha` 0.05 with no depth
limit. Both keep the first of two conflicting colliders.

## Limits

- **Data coverage.** We measure one simulated regime for each kind of data, with mean degree 2 and
  2,000 rows, up to 40 variables. These results do not establish performance on measured data, on
  more categories, or on other mechanisms.
- **Hill climbing against pgmpy.** The two searches maximize the same score, but their graphs
  differ on 28 of 30 datasets: pgmpy's has fewer errors on 17 and Andrey's on 9. On those 17, the
  searches agree on every move until adding `x -> y` and adding `y -> x` tie exactly. Andrey breaks
  that tie by column order, and pgmpy by its own move order; from there, the two reach different
  local optima.
- **PC against bnlearn on categorical data.** bnlearn's `-adf` tests return `p = 1` when a table has
  fewer than 5 rows per cell on average, so its PC removes edges that Andrey's PC tests. At 2,000
  rows, this starts at about four conditioning variables. On the same skeleton, the two also orient
  colliders differently: bnlearn keeps the first separating set it finds, and Andrey the union of
  the sets that separated the pair.
- **Tetrad's PC.** With the conditional Gaussian test, Tetrad stopped with an error on 12 of 30
  datasets, so its speedups use 8, 6, and 4 datasets at 10, 20, and 40 variables. With the
  degenerate Gaussian test, Tetrad's graphs have more errors than the empty graph on most datasets
  from 20 variables, and it exceeded the time limit on half of the datasets at 40. Tetrad's
  degenerate Gaussian test statistic is twice the likelihood ratio.
- **GES with the degenerate Gaussian score.** At 40 variables, Andrey's GES is slower than Tetrad's
  FGES (0.802x).
- **Hill climbing timing.** On categorical data and on mixed data, hill climbing at 10 and 20
  variables takes under 0.1 s in both Andrey and bnlearn, so those sizes show no speedup.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured hardware, core budget, and settings.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. bnlearn runs in the harness's R environment. Tetrad runs through causal-cmd 1.12.0
with the driver described in the guide. To rerun the comparisons, install Andrey 0.6.0
(andrey-core), causal-learn 0.1.4.8, pgmpy 1.1.2, bnlearn 5.2.1, and causal-cmd 1.12.0, then run the
drivers below. Times will differ with the hardware and build; graphs should match closely.

| Comparison | Driver | d | n | Datasets x repeats | Wall cap (s) | Cores |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Categorical data | `discrete_cpu.py` | 10, 20, and 40 | 2,000 | 5 x 1 | 1,800 | 4 |
| Mixed data | `mixed_cpu.py` | 10, 20, and 40 | 2,000 | 10 x 1 | 2,400 | 4 |

```shell
$ANDREY_BENCH_PYTHON benchmarks/discrete_cpu.py --out outputs/discrete-cpu
$ANDREY_BENCH_PYTHON benchmarks/mixed_cpu.py --out outputs/mixed-cpu --cap-mem-mb 30000 \
    --cap-wall-s 2400 --categorical-share 0.5 --categories 2 3
$ANDREY_BENCH_PYTHON benchmarks/mixed_cpu.py --out outputs/mixed-cpu-35 --cap-mem-mb 30000 \
    --cap-wall-s 2400 --categorical-share 0.5 --categories 3 5
```

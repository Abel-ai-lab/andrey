This card maps, for each supported method, the problems it can handle: those it finishes within
the cap and solves with fewer errors than the empty graph. It measures Andrey alone, on the 0.9.0
code, with no other package. Each boundary is a reference for the measured conditions, not a hard
limit; it depends on the data, settings, and hardware.

## Summary

**How far each method goes.** At 2,000 rows and a mean degree of 2, every size up to these finished
and beat the empty graph: BOSS 1,600 variables; GRaSP 800; PC, CALM, and DirectLiNGAM 400; GES,
GIES, and XGES 200 (400 with 10,000 rows); CD-NOD, tabu search, and hill climbing 100; ICA-LiNGAM
50; exact search 22. The [post's table](../../blog/20261106-andrey-0-10-0.md) gives 500 and 10,000
rows, and the maps below every size.

**What stops them.** The score-based searches stop on accuracy, and reach further with more rows.
The permutation-based searches and exact search stop on time. PC stops on accuracy.

## The boundary

**Inside the boundary.** A size, a number of variables with a number of rows and a mean degree, is
inside a method's boundary when it is through, fewer than 3 of its fits cut by the time cap, the
memory cap, or an error, and the median errors of its finished fits are fewer than the empty
graph's. A fit that did not finish never counts as an empty graph. A ladder's boundary is the
largest size up to which every size is inside.

**How a ladder ends.** A ladder steps the number of variables on one setting until it stops: on
time, memory, or an error (3 fits of a size cut), past the accuracy boundary (two sizes in a row
that do not beat the empty graph), or at its last planned size. Larger sizes past the accuracy
boundary cost compute and tell you nothing more. Seven ladders ran past it before the rule, and
keep those sizes, marked.

**What a boundary is not.** Each boundary is a reference measured under the conditions below: our
graph and data generators, the listed sizes and densities, each method's default settings, and the
listed machines. Other graphs, data, settings, or hardware move it. It is not a guarantee that a
method fails past it, or succeeds before it.

## Method

**Data.** Each method runs on the data its release card used: Erdos-Renyi graphs with linear
relations and Gaussian noise for the Gaussian searches, with non-Gaussian noise for the LiNGAM
methods; for RCD and BottomUpParceLiNGAM, one node in five is hidden (at least one), the number of
variables counts the hidden ones, and the errors are against the true PAG of the observed nodes; for
GIES, 40% of the variables get single-variable interventions, with the rows split evenly between
observation and each intervention, so the rows per setting shrink as the variables grow. Each
ladder's label names its setting.

**Two maps.** The size map: variables from 20, doubling, by 500, 2,000, and 10,000 rows, with a
mean degree of 2. The density map: the same variables by a mean degree of 1, 2, 4, and 8, with
2,000 rows. Exact search runs from 10 variables.

**Datasets.** 5 datasets per size, one per seed, 10 where the median errors were within 10% of the
empty graph's, and 20 per size for the pairwise methods. TODO(verify): the sizes with 3 or 4.

**Errors.** The structural Hamming distance against the true graph in the form each method returns
(a CPDAG, a DAG, or a PAG); F1 for VARMA-LiNGAM and LongitudinalLiNGAM; the share of pairs with the
right direction for ANM and PNL; and the clustering agreement for GIN. The empty graph's errors are
the number of true edges.

**Limits.** A one-hour wall cap and a 28,000 MB memory cap per fit, on 4 CPUs: almost all on an AMD
EPYC 7713, some on an AMD EPYC 9534 or an Intel Xeon Gold 6542Y. Nine sizes ran on two CPU models;
their errors are the median over every finished fit, and they quote no time.

## Comparisons with Tetrad

Each table answers one question: on the same datasets, how fast and how accurate is Andrey's PC,
GES, BOSS, GRaSP, and FCI against Tetrad 7.6.10's? Both run with the same score or test, penalty,
and depth, on the same node. TODO(numbers): the tables from the 0.10.0 summary: per method and size,
both times, the speedup, and both errors as the mean SHD over 20 datasets.

Where the errors differ, the cause, from a matched rerun on more datasets:

- **PC.** The two find the same skeleton on every dataset and differ only in how they orient
  colliders: Tetrad keeps the first separating set it finds, and Andrey the union of the sets of
  that size. Andrey makes fewer errors at every size.
- **GES.** At the BIC penalty both packages use by default, both searches return graphs denser than
  the truth on these data, and Andrey's single pass and FGES's three passes stop in different local
  optima. With a penalty of 2 on both sides, the errors are equal within the run-to-run spread.
- **BOSS.** Each package runs one start of a local search: Tetrad from the data's column order,
  Andrey from a seeded order. Each reaches the true graph on some datasets and not others; over 20
  datasets the errors are equal within the run-to-run spread.
- **GRaSP.** A difference in search quality, not in the depth or the score. At 100 variables,
  Andrey's search ends at a worse BIC than the true graph's in 58% to 75% of fits, and Tetrad's in
  45% to 55%, so Andrey makes more errors; the gap sits in the fits that go furthest wrong.

## Limits

- **A reference, not a hard limit.** The boundaries depend on the graph and data generators, the
  densities, each method's settings, and the machine; see [What a boundary is not](#the-boundary).
- **Defaults only.** Every method runs with its default settings. A smaller `alpha`, a
  `max_depth`, or another score can move a boundary either way.
- **The cap.** A longer cap moves a time boundary to the right; it does not move an accuracy
  boundary.

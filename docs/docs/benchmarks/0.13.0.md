# 0.13.0 release card: IAMB and MMPC

IAMB and MMPC are supported methods. We compare each with bnlearn 5.2.1 on the graph it returns,
scored against that graph's own truth: the CPDAG for IAMB's default `structure="cpdag"` and for
MMPC's `structure="cpdag"`, the skeleton for `structure="skeleton"`, and the moral graph for IAMB's
`structure="moral"`, which bnlearn's `learn.mb` blankets joined by the AND rule give.

| MMPC | CPDAG | 12 | 1 | 9 | 0 | 2 | +0.70 [+0.10, +1.55] (d 100, n 1,000, degree 2) | 18.8 |
| MMPC | skeleton | 12 | 0 | 12 | 0 | 0 | +0.60 [-0.40, +1.65] (d 100, n 1,000, degree 4) | 7.6 |

A setting is one size, sample size, and mean degree. D is Andrey's error minus bnlearn's on the
same dataset. A setting with at least 10 paired datasets is worse when the paired 95% bootstrap
interval of the mean D lies wholly above 0, better when wholly below, and a tie otherwise; a
setting with fewer is listed, not judged. The time ratio is bnlearn's fit time over Andrey's, the
median of the settings' median ratios. The worst setting is the worse one where there is one,
else the judged setting with the largest D.

## Where bnlearn makes fewer errors

Two CPDAG settings are worse: IAMB at 50 variables, 5,000 rows, and mean degree 2 (D +0.50, 9.15
errors against 8.65 on average), and MMPC at 100 variables, 1,000 rows, and mean degree 2 (D +0.70,
37.05 against 36.35). We traced every dataset on which the outputs differ. In each, the difference
starts at one node's first grow step (IAMB) or forward step (MMPC). There the p-values of several
candidates underflow to exactly 0 in bnlearn, which takes the first column among them. Andrey takes
the candidate with the strongest test statistic, the association measure of the papers
(Tsamardinos, Aliferis, and Statnikov 2003; Tsamardinos, Brown, and Aliferis 2006), so its result
does not depend on the column order. The choice bnlearn makes, applied in Andrey's search,
reproduces bnlearn's skeleton in 12 of the 12 traced datasets (4 of IAMB, 8 of MMPC). Skeleton
differences carry most of the gap: +7 of IAMB's +10 pairs over 20 datasets, and +12 of MMPC's +14.
The rest is orientation: Meek's rules on a cyclic partially directed graph, a collider that bnlearn
skips for closing a directed cycle, and a triple whose two blankets have equal size, which Andrey
tests on both and bnlearn on one. The skeleton settings of the same cells are ties.

## Method

**Data.** Linear-Gaussian data from Erdos-Renyi graphs, standardized, with 20, 50, and 100
variables, 1,000 and 5,000 rows, and mean degree 2 and 4. Each setting has up to 20 datasets, one
per seed, with the columns in a shuffled order; settings keep at least 10 datasets, and the ones
that bnlearn or Andrey could not finish keep fewer pairs.

**Fits.** One fit at a time on one core, Fisher-z tests at alpha 0.05 (bnlearn `test="zf"`), a
600 s cap per fit, and one untimed warm-up fit except at 5,000 rows, mean degree 4, and 50 or more
variables, where there is none. A fit that reaches the cap is a timeout. It is counted as coverage,
left out of the pairs, and never scored as an empty graph.

**Errors.** The structural Hamming distance of the returned graph to its own truth. The tables give
the median over the paired datasets, for Andrey, bnlearn, and the empty graph; D is the mean of the
per-dataset differences, the statistic of the verdict. A DAG that bnlearn returns is compared
through its CPDAG.

## Limits

The settings cover 20 to 100 variables and 1,000 to 5,000 rows on linear-Gaussian data with
Fisher-z tests. Larger graphs, other data types, and other tests are not measured. At 20 variables,
1,000 rows, and mean degree 4, the median CPDAG of IAMB makes 24 errors and that of MMPC 31.5,
against 40 for the empty graph, the closest of any setting.

Three CPDAG settings have fewer than 10 paired datasets and are not judged. IAMB at 100 variables,
5,000 rows, and mean degree 4 has 9 pairs: bnlearn reached the cap on 11 datasets. MMPC at 100
variables and mean degree 4 has 2 pairs at 1,000 rows and none at 5,000: Andrey reached the cap on
6 and 19 of 20 datasets, and bnlearn on 18 and 20. MMPC's CPDAG tests every subset of the smaller
blanket of each unshielded triple, so its fits slow down as the blankets grow; over all settings
Andrey reached the cap in 25 of 226 CPDAG fits and bnlearn in 51 of 226. IAMB's CPDAG finished
every Andrey fit (bnlearn reached the cap in 12 of 201), and the skeleton and moral graph of both
methods finished every Andrey fit.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey (andrey-core) and bnlearn 5.2.1 with R
4.6.1 in the harness's R environment. Every driver shuffles the columns by default. Times will
differ with the hardware and build; graphs should match closely.

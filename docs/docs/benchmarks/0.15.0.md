# 0.15.0 release card: multi-domain data

:::::{div} release-opening

:::{card}
:class-card: release-cover
:shadow: none

Multi-domain data

0.15.0
:::

::::{div} release-opening-text

This card measures FCI-JCI, BOSS-FCI, and GRaSP-FCI, which 0.15.0 makes supported, against their
authors' code, on data with hidden common causes, FCI-JCI's pooled from an observational regime and
an interventional one. Each result holds for the measured conditions; it depends on the data,
settings, and hardware.

::::
:::::

0.15.0 makes FCI-JCI, BOSS-FCI, and GRaSP-FCI supported, and we compared each with its authors'
implementation, FCI-JCI's in pcalg and the other two in Tetrad, on data simulated the way their
papers do. FCI-JCI learns from data pooled from several environments, with the environment as one
more variable, and allows hidden common causes. BOSS-FCI and GRaSP-FCI allow them too: they start
from the graph of a BOSS or GRaSP search and remove with tests the edges hidden causes explain.

## Summary

| Method | Reference | Comparisons | Better | Tie | Worse | Speed against the reference |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FCI-JCI | [pcalg 2.7-12](https://cran.r-project.org/package=pcalg) (R) | 3 settings x 2 assumption sets x 2 levels | 0 | 12 | 0 | 4.0x to 4.6x faster at 4 variables, 72x to 79x at 10 |
| BOSS-FCI | [Tetrad 7.6.10](https://github.com/cmu-phil/tetrad) (Java) | 36 settings x 2 levels, and 100 variables | 1 | 72 | 0 | 1.05x to 5.8x faster at 20 variables, 1.85x at 100 |
| GRaSP-FCI | [Tetrad 7.6.10](https://github.com/cmu-phil/tetrad) (Java) | 36 settings x 2 levels, and 100 variables | 1 | 72 | 0 | 1.16x to 5.8x faster at 20 variables, 7.6x at 100 |

Andrey ties the reference in all 12 FCI-JCI comparisons and in 144 of the 146 BOSS-FCI and
GRaSP-FCI ones, and is better in the other two.

BOSS-FCI and GRaSP-FCI also take `edge_removal="triangles"`, an option that tests only the edges in
a triangle of the CPDAG for removal. With it, Andrey follows the same published rule as Tetrad
7.6.3, the GFCI paper's (Ogarrio, Spirtes, and Ramsey 2016, Sec. 5), so we compared the option with
Tetrad 7.6.3 on the same datasets and settings:

| Method | Option | Reference | Comparisons | Better | Tie | Worse |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BOSS-FCI | `edge_removal="triangles"` | [Tetrad 7.6.3](https://github.com/cmu-phil/tetrad) (Java) | 36 settings x 2 levels, and 100 variables | 1 | 72 | 0 |
| GRaSP-FCI | `edge_removal="triangles"` | [Tetrad 7.6.3](https://github.com/cmu-phil/tetrad) (Java) | 36 settings x 2 levels, and 100 variables | 2 | 71 | 0 |

With the option, Andrey ties Tetrad 7.6.3 in 143 of the 146 comparisons and is better in the other
three. Here is how to read the tables:

- The speed is the median, over the datasets, of the reference's fit time divided by Andrey's on the
  same dataset, written as how many times faster or slower Andrey is.
- A comparison is better or worse when the 95% interval of the mean paired difference in errors lies
  entirely below or above 0. One with fewer than 10 paired datasets is reported and not judged.
- A better or worse result has to hold twice. It counts only where a rerun on fresh seeds agreed,
  and one on the first seeds alone counts as a tie. No FCI-JCI comparison came out better or worse,
  so none was rerun; 12 BOSS-FCI and GRaSP-FCI comparisons did, 6 each way, and 2 of them
  held on the rerun. With the option against Tetrad 7.6.3, 19 did, 17 better and 2 worse, and 3
  better ones held.
- Andrey must also beat doing nothing wherever the reference does, making fewer errors than the
  empty graph. Where the reference does not beat it either, the floor is reported and not judged.
  The floor is judged on the first seeds; a rerun's floor only confirms a failure there.

## How we measured

Every dataset was simulated the way the method's paper simulates its data and given to both
packages, so each comparison pairs Andrey's error with the reference's on the same data. D in the
tables is the mean of those paired differences, Andrey's error minus the reference's, with its 95%
bootstrap interval.

- FCI-JCI ran one fit at a time on one core of an AMD EPYC 7713 with one BLAS thread, and BOSS-FCI
  and GRaSP-FCI the same way on an AMD EPYC 7713 or 9534, and out of the claim at mean degree 10 on
  an Intel Xeon Gold 6542Y.
- Each fit had 1,800 s, imports and warm-up included, and BOSS-FCI's and GRaSP-FCI's 3,600 s at 100
  variables; a fit past that is a timeout, never an empty graph.
- Both packages run at the comparison's settings, given in each method's section below.

## FCI-JCI

FCI-JCI (Mooij, Magliacane, and Claassen 2020) runs FCI on data pooled from several environments,
with the environment as one more variable that no data variable causes. We compared it with pcalg's
`fci` with `jci`, the implementation by the method's authors, on their simulation. All 12
comparisons tie, and Andrey is 4 to 79 times faster.

- The data follow the paper's simulation (Sec. 5.2): linear Gaussian equations with hidden common
  causes, and one binary context variable that changes one variable, in an observational regime and
  an interventional one.
- The claim holds the paper's small setting, 4 variables with mechanism changes and with perfect
  interventions, and its larger one, 10 variables with mechanism changes. Each has 500 rows per
  regime, with 20 datasets per setting.
- Both packages run the Fisher z test at alpha 0.01, the paper's level, and at 0.05, Andrey's
  default, under two of the paper's assumption sets: `confounded_index=True` with pcalg's
  `jci = "1"`, and `False` with `"123"`.
- We count the endpoint SHD over the data variables and the environment against FCI-JCI's PAG at the
  true distribution, built from the true graph and checked against pcalg's on every dataset.

| 4 variables, mechanism change | 1 | 0.01 | 5.95 | 5.90 | 12.50 | +0.05 [-0.30, +0.45] |
| 4 variables, mechanism change | 123 | 0.01 | 5.90 | 5.80 | 12.50 | +0.10 [+0.00, +0.30] |
| 4 variables, mechanism change | 1 | 0.05 | 5.05 | 5.05 | 12.50 | +0.00 [-0.15, +0.15] |
| 4 variables, mechanism change | 123 | 0.05 | 5.00 | 5.05 | 12.50 | -0.05 [-0.15, +0.00] |
| 4 variables, perfect intervention | 1 | 0.01 | 6.05 | 6.15 | 12.50 | -0.10 [-0.25, +0.00] |
| 4 variables, perfect intervention | 123 | 0.01 | 6.05 | 6.15 | 12.50 | -0.10 [-0.25, +0.00] |
| 4 variables, perfect intervention | 1 | 0.05 | 5.45 | 5.40 | 12.50 | +0.05 [-0.15, +0.30] |
| 4 variables, perfect intervention | 123 | 0.05 | 5.45 | 5.40 | 12.50 | +0.05 [-0.15, +0.30] |
| 10 variables, mechanism change | 1 | 0.01 | 37.85 | 37.80 | 54.80 | +0.05 [-0.90, +0.95] |

Both packages make fewer errors than the empty graph in every comparison, so the floor applies in
all 12, and Andrey passes each. The two returned the same PAG on 18 or 19 of the 20 datasets at 4
variables and on 4 to 6 at 10. There they differ by design in how they pick separating sets and
possible-d-separation sets: Andrey's result does not depend on the column order, and pcalg's stable
skeleton (Colombo and Maathuis 2014) keeps the first separating set it finds in column order.

The paper's large setting, 100 variables with 100 rows per regime, is in the claim where pcalg
finishes, and pcalg did not: on the two datasets of a dry run, every pcalg fit ran past 1,800 s, so
the campaign ran Andrey alone there. Each of Andrey's fits took under half a second, and it made
fewer errors than the empty graph on every dataset, 328 against 412 on average at alpha 0.01.

Out of the claim, with the paper's several context variables merged into one environment, and with
100 or 2,000 rows per regime at 10 variables, no comparison lies above or below 0. At 20 variables
pcalg finished 4 and 6 of the 10 datasets within 1,800 s, too few to judge, and at 50 variables
neither dataset of the dry run. Andrey finished every dataset of the campaign.

At 10 variables and alpha 0.01, FCI on the pooled rows or on the observational rows alone made
more errors over the data variables than FCI-JCI, and so did CD-NOD, which assumes no hidden common
cause.

At 4 variables Andrey's median fit took 0.004 to 0.005 s against pcalg's 0.017 to 0.021 s, and at
10 variables 0.011 to 0.013 s against 0.83 to 1.31 s, counting pcalg's time from the transfer into
R. Over the paired datasets the median ratio ran from 4.0 to 4.6 at 4 variables and from 72 to 79
at 10.

## BOSS-FCI

BOSS-FCI (Ramsey, Andrews, and Spirtes 2025) runs BOSS's search for a CPDAG, removes with
conditional-independence tests the adjacencies that hidden common causes explain, and orients the
rest with FCI's rules, as GFCI does with GES's graph. We compared it with Tetrad 7.6.10's `BossFci`,
the implementation by the method's authors, on their simulation. It ties in 72 of 73 comparisons and
is better in one, at 100 variables, and it is 1.05 to 5.8 times faster at 20 variables and 1.85
times at 100.

- The data follow the paper's simulation (Sec. 8.1.1): linear Gaussian equations on random forward
  DAGs, with up to 0, 4, or 8 of the nodes with two or more children hidden.
- The claim holds the paper's settings: 20 measured variables at mean degree 2, 4, or 6 with 200 to
  5,000 rows, and 100 measured variables with 10 hidden, mean degree 6, 1,000 rows, and
  conditioning sets of at most 7, with 20 datasets each.
- Both packages run the BIC score and Fisher's z test at the paper's penalty discount 2 and alpha
  0.01, and at Andrey's defaults, 1 and 0.05. Tetrad tests the edges in an unseeded order, so it
  runs five times per dataset and its error there is the mean of the five.
- We count the endpoint SHD against the PAG of the measured variables, built from the true graph and
  checked against pcalg's on every graph of up to 30 measured variables.

Means over the comparisons of each group, pooled over the hidden counts and the four sizes at 20
variables; the [comparison page][bgf] lists every comparison.

Both packages make fewer errors than the empty graph in every comparison, so the floor applies in
all 73, and Andrey passes each. Three comparisons came out worse and three better on seeds 0 to
19, each by at most 4.3 errors; rerun on seeds 100 to 119, the worse ones and the better ones at
20 variables tie, and the one at 100 variables held: D was -4.3 [-8.32, -0.57] on the first seeds
and -5.01 [-7.68, -2.36] on the rerun. Andrey returned the same PAG as one of Tetrad's runs on 475
of the 1,460 datasets. They differ by design in how they remove edges: Andrey runs FCI's adjacency
search level by level, so its result does not depend on the order of the edges, and Tetrad tests
each edge once in a random order.

Tetrad 7.6.3, the authors' release in causal-cmd 1.12.0, tests only the edges that lie in a
triangle of the CPDAG, the rule of the GFCI paper (Ogarrio, Spirtes, and Ramsey 2016, Sec. 5),
rather than Algorithm 5's every edge. It keeps more edges. At the paper's settings it made fewer
errors at degree 4 and 6, 53.6 and 119.3 on average against Andrey's 56.67 and 122.94, and at 100
variables, 583.8 against 636.65; at Andrey's defaults at degree 2 it made more, 24.02 against 20.99.

With `edge_removal="triangles"`, Andrey follows the same rule as Tetrad 7.6.3, and against it on the
same datasets it ties in 72 of 73 comparisons and is better in one. At the paper's settings it made
53.3 and 118.52 errors on average at degree 4 and 6, against Tetrad 7.6.3's 53.6 and 119.3, and
579.75 against 583.8 at 100 variables; at the defaults at degree 2 it made 23.61 against 24.02.
Eight comparisons came out better and two worse on seeds 0 to 19; rerun on seeds 100 to 119, the
worse ones tie, and one better one held: at the defaults, degree 2 with 8 hidden and 1,000 rows, D
was -1.55 [-2.7, -0.45] and then -1.4 [-3.15, -0.15]. The floor applies in 72 comparisons, and
Andrey passes each; at the defaults at degree 2 with 8 hidden and 200 rows neither Tetrad 7.6.3
(38.45 errors) nor the option (38.55) beats the empty graph (37.8). Andrey returned the same PAG as
Tetrad 7.6.3 on 343 of the 1,460 datasets: the rule is the same, and they still differ in how they
search for separating sets and where BOSS starts. With the option, Andrey's median fit took 0.012
to 0.41 s at 20 variables and 2.5 s at 100, against Tetrad 7.6.3's 0.041 to 3.0 s and 24.2 s, timed
in separate runs on AMD EPYC 7713 or 9534 processors.

At 20 variables Andrey's median fit took 0.014 to 0.49 s against Tetrad 7.6.10's 0.058 to 0.5 s,
counting Tetrad's search inside the JVM, and at 100 variables 1.42 s against 2.6 s. Over the paired
datasets the median ratio ran from 1.05 to 5.8 at 20 variables and was 1.85 at 100.

## GRaSP-FCI

GRaSP-FCI runs the same steps with GRaSP's search in place of BOSS's. Against Tetrad 7.6.10's
`GraspFci` on the same datasets and settings it ties in 72 of 73 comparisons and is better in one,
and it is 1.16 to 5.8 times faster at 20 variables and 7.6 times at 100.

The floor applies in all 73 comparisons, and Andrey passes each. Three comparisons came out worse
and three better on seeds 0 to 19; rerun on seeds 100 to 119, the worse ones tie, and one better
one held: at the paper's settings, degree 6 with no hidden variable and 1,000 rows, D was -2.49

With `edge_removal="triangles"`, against Tetrad 7.6.3, Andrey's GRaSP-FCI ties in 71 of 73
comparisons and is better in two: at the paper's settings, degree 6 with no hidden variable and

and at 100 variables 1.3 s against 11.9 s. At 200 measured variables with 20 hidden, out of the
claim, one dataset took Andrey 10 s and Tetrad 502 s with GRaSP-FCI, and 9.3 s against 16.9 s
with BOSS-FCI.

Out of the claim, at 100 measured variables with mean degree 10, both hybrids made about 21 fewer
errors than Tetrad 7.6.10 on 10 datasets and about 80 more than Tetrad 7.6.3. On the setting of
Andrey's recovery test, 20 measured variables with 4 hidden, every comparison ties but BOSS-FCI's at
the defaults, lower by 3.9.

## Limits

Each result holds for the settings above. These are the places where the method, or both packages,
fall short:

- FCI-JCI takes one environment variable. The paper's designs with several context variables ran
  here merged into one regime number, out of the claim. There Andrey ties pcalg too, and reading the
  regime number as a category (`indep_test="cond_gauss"`) made fewer errors over the data variables
  and the environment at alpha 0.01: 6.80 against 7.75 at 4 variables and 44.50 against 49.10 at
  10, from the environment's edges.
- At 100 variables with 100 rows per regime, pcalg finished no fit within 1,800 s, so there is no
  reference to compare with. Andrey's errors there are measured only against the empty graph's.
- BOSS-FCI and GRaSP-FCI recover only part of the PAG on their paper's simulation, and so does
  Tetrad: the hidden nodes leave many pairs joined only through them. At degree 6 their errors were
  about three quarters of the empty graph's, and at degree 2 with 8 hidden and 200 rows at the
  defaults 34.35 and 33.3 against the empty graph's 37.8.
- At the paper's settings, the GFCI paper's rule of testing only the edges in a triangle of the
  CPDAG, which Tetrad 7.6.3 runs, made fewer errors than Algorithm 5 at degree 4 and 6 and at 100
  variables; at Andrey's defaults on sparse graphs it made more. Andrey's default and Tetrad 7.6.10
  follow Algorithm 5, and `edge_removal="triangles"` follows the GFCI paper's rule.
- Tetrad 7.6.10's rules R5 and R9 read a covered path as uncovered, so they can put a tail where no
  Markov-equivalent MAG has one, a defect of the reference. Its tail precision here was close to
  Andrey's.

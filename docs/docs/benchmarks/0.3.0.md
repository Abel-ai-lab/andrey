::::{div} release-opening-text

We compare three score-based searches with packages that implement them: XGES with xges, and Tabu
search and hill climbing with bnlearn. XGES returns the same graph as xges on 40 of 40 datasets,
with or without its extended search, and runs 3.95-140x faster with it. Tabu search and hill
climbing return the same equivalence class as bnlearn on 31 of 35 and 30 of 34 datasets. Up to
`d = 200`, they run 2.56-34.4x and 3.48-30.5x faster; at `d = 1,600`, where every bnlearn fit hit
its 1-hour cap, at least 474x and 609x.

Graph agreement does not ensure accuracy. From `d = 50`, Tabu search and hill climbing return graphs
with more errors than the empty graph's in both packages, at this sample size of `n = 10d` (see
[Known limits](#known-limits)). You can find speedups and accuracy in the [summary](#summary), and
measurement and scoring definitions in [how to read the results](#how-to-read-the-results). These
results apply to the measured workloads; speed and accuracy depend on the data, settings, and
hardware.

## How to read the results

**Data.** We evaluate every method on `linear_gauss_er`: Erdos-Renyi directed acyclic graphs (DAGs)
with linear mechanisms, Gaussian noise, standardized data, and `n = 10d` samples. XGES runs at mean
degrees 2 and 4, on `d = 25, 50, 100, and 200` with both packages and on `d = 500 and 1,000` with
Andrey only. Tabu search and hill climbing run at mean degree 2 on
`d = 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800` with both packages, on `d = 1,600` with bnlearn capped at 1
hour, and on `d = 3,200` with Andrey only. There are five seeds per size and mean degree.

**Accuracy.** We score each graph by its structural Hamming distance (SHD) to the true completed
partially directed acyclic graph (CPDAG); a DAG, as bnlearn returns, is scored through its CPDAG.
Lower is better. The empty graph's SHD is the number of true edges. "Same graph" means equal
`structure_hash` values: both packages return CPDAGs with the same edges and the same marks. For
Tabu search and hill climbing, "same class" means the same Markov equivalence class, with the same
skeleton and v-structures, because bnlearn returns DAGs and Andrey returns CPDAGs. A fit that hit
its cap has no time, graph, or score, so a size's graph columns cover the datasets both packages
finished.

**BIC.** We rescore every graph with Andrey's BIC on the data it was fitted on, in deviance units,
so both packages are compared on one scale; lower is better. For XGES, the BIC gap is the returned
graph's BIC minus the true graph's: below zero, the returned graph scores better than the truth. For
Tabu search and hill climbing, "BIC vs hill climbing" is the fit's BIC minus Andrey hill climbing's
on the same dataset: below zero, the fit scores better than hill climbing. SHD and BIC are medians
over datasets.

**Timing.** Times are seconds per fit, the median over datasets of each dataset's median over its
repeats. XGES fits once per dataset; Tabu search and hill climbing fit three times per dataset at
`d = 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200` and once from `d = 400`. For each dataset, we divide the package's
median time by Andrey's, then report the median of these paired ratios. This need not equal the
ratio of the displayed median times. Medians cover the datasets both packages finished. A speedup
marked $\geq$ is a lower bound, because some bnlearn fits hit their cap. Each xges fit, and each
Tabu search and hill-climbing fit in both packages, follows one untimed warm-up fit; Tabu search and
hill-climbing fits have a 30-minute cap. A row's footnote gives any exception. Times and ratios have
three significant figures and BIC values two decimals, as in the summary. **TODO(0.3.0):** confirm
the warm-up fits before Andrey's timed XGES fits.

**Hardware.** Every fit ran on an AMD EPYC 7713 64-Core Processor: 1 core per fit for XGES and 4
cores for Tabu search and hill climbing. Ratios apply within this campaign and do not transfer
between machines.

## Score-based methods

Every fit in a comparison searches under the same BIC penalty. Tabu search runs with bnlearn's
default tabu settings in both packages. In the XGES comparison, no search limits a variable's
parents, Andrey GES included.

### XGES

XGES searches the equivalence classes that GES searches, with GES's moves and the turning move of
GIES, in a different order. Each step takes the best improving deletion, else the best reversal of a
directed edge, else the best addition; the base search stops when no move improves the score. The
extended search then deletes each edge of that graph in turn, forbids adding it back in that
direction, and reruns the base search, keeping any result with a better score.

### Tabu search

visited DAG. It returns the best-scoring DAG it visits; Andrey returns that DAG's CPDAG. Until it

hill climbing's.

## Known limits

- **Data coverage.** We measure one regime: linear-Gaussian Erdos-Renyi graphs, standardized, with
  `n = 10d` samples. XGES runs at mean degrees 2 and 4 up to `d = 1,000`, and Tabu search and hill
  climbing at mean degree 2 up to `d = 3,200`. These results do not establish performance across all
  data regimes.
- **Tabu search and hill climbing against the empty graph.** From `d = 50`, the median SHD of both
  methods is above the empty graph's in both packages. At `d = 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800`, Tabu
  search has 54, 117, 267, 615, and 1,495 in Andrey and 55, 117, 267, 615, and 1,495 in bnlearn, and
  hill climbing 55, 120, 273, 605, and 1,483.5 and 54, 120, 273, 605, and 1,483.5, against 50, 100,
  200, 400, and 800 for the empty graph. At `d = 1,600 and 3,200`, Andrey's Tabu search has 3,754
  and 9,312 and its hill climbing 3,752 and 9,306, against 1,600 and 3,200. At `d = 10` both equal
  it (10 against 10). The packages return the same equivalence class on most datasets, so these
  results reflect the methods at this sample size, not differences between implementations.
- **XGES against the empty graph.** At mean degree 2 and `d = 500 and 1,000`, which only Andrey ran,
  XGES's median SHD is above the empty graph's: 671 and 1,758 with the extended search and 663 and
  1,688 without it, against 500 and 1,000. Its BIC gap there is below zero, so at this sample size
  the BIC score itself prefers these graphs to the true one. At every size both packages ran, XGES's
  median SHD is below the empty graph's.
- **XGES fit times.** With the extended search, XGES's median fit at `d = 1,000` takes 51.6 s at
  mean degree 2 and 289 s at mean degree 4 on 1 core; without it, 1.54 s and 5.15 s. The extended
  search reruns the base search for each edge it tries to delete, so its time grows with the number
  of edges.
- **Shared nodes.** Andrey's sub-second Tabu search and hill-climbing fits ran on shared nodes, so
  their speedups are approximate, and Andrey's times at `d = 400 and 800` include interference on a
  shared node. The speedup ranges leave those sizes out (see the footnotes).
- **Capped fits.** Every bnlearn fit for Tabu search at `d = 1,600` hit its 1-hour cap, one of five
  bnlearn fits for hill climbing at `d = 800` hit its 30-minute cap, and every bnlearn fit for hill
  climbing at `d = 1,600` hit its 1-hour cap, so those speedups are lower bounds: a capped fit has
  no time, graph, or score.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured hardware, core budget, and settings.

## Reproduce

The [benchmark harness guide][harness] documents environment setup, the campaign drivers, and the
scores. To rerun the comparisons, install Andrey 0.3.0 (andrey-core), xges 0.1.6 in its own
environment, and bnlearn 5.2.1 on R 4.6.1, then run the drivers with the settings below. Times will
differ with the hardware and build; graphs and scores should match closely.

| Comparison | Driver | d | Mean degree | Seeds x repeats | Warm-up fits | Wall cap (s) | Cores |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| XGES, XGES without the extended search | `xges_cpu.py` | 25-1,000 | 2, 4 | 5 x 1 | TODO | TODO | 1 |
| Tabu search, hill climbing | `tabu_cpu.py` | 10-200; 400-800 | 2 | 5 x 3; 5 x 1 | 1 | 1,800 | 4 |
| Tabu search, hill climbing, larger sizes | `tabu_cpu.py` | 1,600; 3,200 | 2 | 5 x 1 | 0 (bnlearn) | 3,600 (bnlearn) | 4 |

The XGES driver runs the xges arms in their own interpreter, named by `ANDREY_BENCH_XGES_PYTHON`.

::::{div} release-opening-text

This card measures the methods 0.14.0 makes supported against their authors' code: GnIES, UT-IGSP,
and IGSP on simulated data mixing observation and experiments, ICP on environments that leave one
variable's mechanism alone, CAM-UV on nonlinear data with hidden variables, and DYNOTEARS on vector
autoregressions. Each result holds for the measured conditions; it depends on the data, settings,
and hardware.

::::
:::::

GnIES, UT-IGSP, IGSP, ICP, CAM-UV, and DYNOTEARS are supported methods. We compare GnIES with gnies
0.3.3, the package of its authors, on linear-Gaussian data whose environments change the noise
variance of their targets, with the targets unknown to both. UT-IGSP is compared with the authors'
library (graphical-model-learning 0.1a8) on data that mix an observational environment with five
experiments whose targets are known in part or not at all, and IGSP with the same library on the
settings of its papers, whose targets are known. ICP is compared with the authors' R package, CAM-UV
with lingam 1.13.0 and causal-learn 0.1.4.8 on nonlinear additive data with hidden variables, and
DYNOTEARS with CausalNex 0.12.2, the implementation its authors released, on the vector
autoregressions of its paper.

## Summary

| Method | Reference | Comparisons | Better | Tie | Worse | Speed against the reference |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GnIES | gnies 0.3.3 | 21 settings x 2 errors | 3 | 39 | 0 | 34x faster |
| UT-IGSP | graphical-model-learning 0.1a8 | 56 settings x 2 errors | 1 | 111 | 0 | 1.7x slower |
| ICP | InvariantCausalPrediction 0.8 (R) | 3 settings x 2 tests | 0 | 6 | 0 | 41x to 760x faster |
| CAM-UV | lingam 1.13.0, causal-learn 0.1.4.8 | 9 settings x 3 references | 0 | 27 | 0 | 2.1x to 3.1x faster than lingam, 7.4x to 13.5x than causal-learn |
| DYNOTEARS | CausalNex 0.12.2 | 38 settings | 5 | 32 | 1 | 1.1x to 3.4x faster in CPU time |
| IGSP | graphical-model-learning 0.1a8 | 87 settings, 114 comparisons | 2 | 112 | 0 | 1.3x faster to 8.0x slower |

The speed is the median, over the datasets, of the reference's fit time over Andrey's on the same
dataset, written as how many times faster or slower Andrey is. A comparison is better or worse when
the 95% interval of the mean paired difference lies entirely below or above 0. Better counts only
where a rerun on fresh seeds, which played no part in choosing the default, agreed; a comparison
better on the first seeds alone counts as a tie. 17 more UT-IGSP comparisons were lower on the first
seeds and on the seeds that chose its default; those seeds are not fresh, so the 17 count as ties.
Andrey must also make fewer errors than the empty estimate, the empty graph or for a target error
no targets beyond those given, wherever the reference does; elsewhere that floor is reported, not
judged.

## Method

**Data.** Simulated with `andrey.data`, in the settings of each method's paper. GnIES: Erdos-Renyi
DAGs on 10 variables, linear equations, Gaussian noise, an observational environment and
environments that each set the noise variance of their targets, 10 to 1,000 rows per environment,
20 datasets per setting, standardized on the pooled rows. UT-IGSP: Erdos-Renyi DAGs on 20
variables with mean degree 1.5, an observational environment and 5 experiments of 1,000 to 5,000
rows each, each on one designed target and 0 to 3 off-targets the fit is not told, by six kinds of
intervention (mean shifts, perfect interventions, variance changes, and weight changes), 20
datasets per setting with 5 draws each. IGSP: the 7-variable DAG of Wang et al. (2017) and
Erdos-Renyi DAGs on 10 or 20 variables with mean degree 1.5, linear equations, Gaussian noise, an
observational environment and settings of known targets by perfect, inhibiting, or imperfect
interventions, 1,000 to 100,000 rows per setting, 20 datasets per setting with 5 draws each. ICP:
linear Gaussian equations on 5 to 40 variables as in the ICP paper's Appendix G, two environments of
100 to 500 rows, and do-interventions on 6 or 10 variables in 3 or 4 environments of 200 rows.
CAM-UV: nonlinear additive effects on 4 to 50 observed variables with 500 to 2,000 rows, in the
paper's simulation, with one node in five or in ten hidden, or with none hidden, 20 datasets per
setting. DYNOTEARS: linear vector autoregressions with one lag from the DYNOTEARS paper,
Erdos-Renyi or Barabasi-Albert graphs on 5 to 100 variables, Gaussian or exponential noise, 50 or
500 steps, 20 series per setting.

**Fits.** GnIES, UT-IGSP, and IGSP: one fit at a time on one core of an AMD EPYC 7713 with one BLAS
thread. ICP, CAM-UV, and DYNOTEARS: four fits at a time, each on one core of an Intel Core i7-7820X
with one thread. GnIES and UT-IGSP run each package at its defaults; Andrey's defaults were chosen
on seeds of their own before the comparison. The other references run at the comparison's settings:
IGSP's library at its defaults but for the level; ICP in both packages at alpha 0.05, testing every
set on every row; CAM-UV's references on sets of up to 3 variables, the paper's size and Andrey's
default, and lingam also at its default of 2; DYNOTEARS in both packages at the paper's settings for
each length, CausalNex fitting without a threshold and then keeping the weights above the paper's
thresholds.

**Errors.** GnIES, UT-IGSP, and IGSP: the structural Hamming distance (SHD) of the returned
interventional essential graph to the true one; GnIES and UT-IGSP also the target error, the targets
missed plus the targets named wrongly. ICP: the target's parents missed plus the variables named
wrongly as parents. CAM-UV: the directed-edge errors against the edges CAM-UV can identify as
direct, 2 for a reversed edge. DYNOTEARS: the SHD over the same-step and the lagged edges. D is the
mean of the paired differences, per dataset, fit, or series, Andrey's error minus the reference's,
with its 95% bootstrap interval.

## Speed

GnIES took a median 0.030 times the reference's fit time over the 420 in-claim datasets (quartiles
0.027 and 0.033), about 34 times faster; at 10 variables Andrey's median fit took 0.29 to 1.33 s
per setting, the reference's 9.1 to 48.6 s.

UT-IGSP is slower than the authors' library at 20 variables: its median fit took 0.24 s against the
library's 0.14 s, a median ratio of 1.68 per dataset (quartiles 1.47 and 1.93). At 50 variables it
took 1.7 s against 0.9 s, and at 100 variables 15.2 s against 16.8 s. A later change in this
release makes UT-IGSP's preparation of the rows about 1.2 times faster on large data (one core, 20
variables, 5 single-target experiments); these comparisons were not rerun after it.

CAM-UV ran 2.1 to 3.1 times faster than lingam and 7.4 to 13.5 times faster than causal-learn,
each searching sets of up to 3 variables, and 1.9 to 3.2 times faster than lingam at its default
set size of 2; at 50 observed variables its median fit took 91 s against lingam's 296 s and
causal-learn's 964 s.

ICP's median fit took 0.010 to 0.24 s with the approximate test at 9 to 15 variables and 1,000
rows, against R's 0.72 to 58 s, 72 to 241 times faster; with the exact test, which reads the same
matrices in Andrey and solves a system per environment and set in R, 458 to 1,554 times faster.
Over the paired fits the median ratio ran from 41 to 760.

IGSP is slower than the authors' library in 84 of the 87 settings of its claim: per setting, its
median fit took 0.74 to 8.0 times as long, a median of 1.8 times at 1,000 rows per setting, 4.3 at
10,000, and 3.5 at 100,000, and at most about 2 s. These times were measured at alpha 1e-3 before
the faster row preparation in this release, and were not rerun after it. Out of claim, at 100
variables and 1,000 rows, the library finished 1 of 10 datasets within 3,600 s and used up to 72 GB
of memory; Andrey took a median 33 s and at most 0.5 GB.

DYNOTEARS used 1.1 to 3.4 times less CPU time than CausalNex, per setting the median ratio over the
paired series, on one core of an Intel Core i7-7820X; at 100 variables and 500 steps its median fit
took 299 s against CausalNex's 393 s.

### ICP

ICP returns the parents of one target, so the comparison is on the same fits: R's
InvariantCausalPrediction 0.8 and Andrey fit the same datasets at alpha 0.05, testing every set of
the other variables on every row. With two environments the approximate test accepted the same
sets as R on all 5,220 paired fits. Every other difference, 2,112 fits, traces to one of two
choices, each rebuilt from the rows: with three or more environments Andrey corrects the
approximate test by the number of environments, `K`, as the paper does, where R uses `K - 1`, and
R's exact test uses a larger residual variance. Pooled over each setting's sizes, as in the table,
every comparison ties. Split by size, one approximate-test row is worse: with do-interventions on 6
variables, the `K` correction gives Andrey 0.024 more errors per fit (interval +0.002 to +0.046),
all from parents it does not name. Andrey follows the paper's `K` there, so the row is allowlisted
as correct by the paper. One exact-test row is lower: on 9 variables with two environments, Andrey
makes 0.012 fewer errors per fit (interval -0.022 to -0.003), on one set of seeds and not rerun.

In the claim, where the environments leave the target's own mechanism alone, the FWER stays well
below alpha in every setting, at most 0.0123, and no scenario's FWER is above alpha beyond sampling

### CAM-UV

In the claim: nonlinear additive effects with hidden variables, or none. The verdict reads the
directed-edge errors against the edges CAM-UV can identify as direct. Every setting ties each
reference once replicated, lingam at its default set size included; the two settings of the
paper's simulation were better on seeds 0 to 19 and tied on seeds 20 to 39.

With one hidden variable in five, no implementation beats the empty graph. At 8 observed and 2
hidden variables, Andrey made 1.50 more errors per graph than the empty graph on seeds 0 to 19
(interval +0.30 to +2.75) and 1.20 more on seeds 20 to 40 (-0.20 to +2.60); lingam and causal-learn
made 1.85 to 1.90 more and 1.20 to 1.30 more. The empty graph is a floor only for a setting where
the reference's own interval against it lies below 0, so these settings are judged against the
references alone.

Andrey's graph differs from lingam's on 217 datasets. We traced 202 of them, every one below 50
observed variables, to five documented choices: the visiting order, the HSIC test, the regressions,
phase 2, and the cycle drop. The other 15, at 50 observed variables, are not traced.

### DYNOTEARS

In the claim: linear vector autoregressions with one lag, from the DYNOTEARS paper, at the paper's
settings for 500 and for 50 steps. At 500 steps every setting whose noise has mean 0 ties, with an
F1 of at least 0.968 for both packages. With exponential noise of mean 1, as the paper's simulations
draw it, CausalNex cannot fit the series' mean, and Andrey made fewer errors at every size at 500
steps and at 5 variables at 50 steps. On seeds 20 to 39, five of those six were better again;
at 100 variables and 500 steps the rerun's interval reaches 0, so that setting counts as a tie.

One setting is worse: exponential noise of mean 0 at 100 variables and 50 steps, by 3.20 errors per
series on seeds 0 to 19 and 4.15 on seeds 20 to 39 (interval +1.30 to +7.10). It comes from
centering: Andrey fits each column around its own mean, the same as an intercept, which the paper's
loss leaves out. Refitted without centering, Andrey ties CausalNex there once rerun. Centering costs
errors on short series whose noise has mean 0, and removes 2.3 to 11.1 errors per series at 500
steps when the noise has mean 1.

### IGSP

In the claim: linear Gaussian data with perfect, inhibiting, and imperfect interventions whose
targets are known, at the sizes of Wang et al. (2017) and Yang et al. (2018). Where a setting has
two or more targets, Andrey is also compared with a copy of the library whose I-contradicting test
follows Definition 4.2 of Yang et al.; the library's own test inverts that definition's conditions.

Andrey's default level is one over the observational rows, as UT-IGSP's. At its defaults, on fresh
seeds, it is worse in none of 114 comparisons and lower in 17. Two of those are better: at 1,000
rows the default level is 1e-3, so seeds 0 to 19 at that level measured the same comparison, and
they agreed. At three fixed levels with both packages at the same one, no row of 684 is worse once
rerun, and 13 are better.

## Other changes in 0.14.0

The [changelog](../changelog.md) measures the other changes: the interventional simulator
(`SCM.do`, `hard`, `soft`, and `envs=`), `env` and `targets` on every method that learns from
environments (`gies`'s `interventions` and `cdnod`'s `domain_index` still work in this release, with
a deprecation warning), the tie rank that ignores offsets, and the faster categorical tests.

## Limits

GnIES's settings cover 10 variables, linear-Gaussian data, and environments that change only the
noise variance of their targets. With 10 rows per environment neither package beats the empty graph
in every setting: in B10 neither does, and in D10 Andrey failed the floor on the rerun seeds. Hard
interventions, mean shifts, weight changes, non-Gaussian noise, nonlinear effects, and 20 to 100
variables lie outside the claim and are reported in the comparison page, not judged.

UT-IGSP's settings cover 20 variables and 5 experiments of 1,000 to 5,000 rows. At 50 variables
with mean degree 5 and 3 experiments, neither package beats the empty graph. With 100 rows per
experiment and no off-targets, Andrey names false targets more often than the library.

CAM-UV's settings cover 4 to 50 observed variables and 500 to 2,000 rows. With one variable in
five hidden, no package makes fewer errors than the empty graph: at 8 observed and 2 hidden
variables Andrey makes 1.50 more on seeds 0 to 19 (interval +0.30 to +2.75) and 1.20 more on seeds
20 to 40 (interval -0.20 to +2.60), and lingam and causal-learn 1.85 to 1.90 and 1.20 to 1.30
more. With nothing hidden every marked pair is
false, and each package marks 3.2 to 3.6 pairs per graph.

ICP's settings cover linear Gaussian data with 5 to 40 variables and 2 to 4 environments of 100 to
500 rows. Its guarantee needs environments that leave the target's mechanism alone: where they do
not, the FWER reaches 0.18, in R too, so pass the known targets. A hidden parent of the target
raised the FWER to 0.018, and 0.145 in one scenario of 20. With at most 3 parents searched on 40
variables, it found almost none of them.

DYNOTEARS's settings cover linear vector autoregressions with one lag, 5 to 100 variables, and 50
or 500 steps, at the paper's settings for each length. With noise of mean 0, centering costs errors
on short series: at 100 variables and 50 steps, about 3 per series more than CausalNex. At its
defaults on 50 steps, both packages make more errors than the empty window from 20 variables on.

IGSP's settings cover linear Gaussian data on 7 to 20 variables with known targets and 1,000 to
100,000 rows per setting. Out of claim, with 100 rows per setting on 10 variables, Andrey made 0.60
more errors than the library on one set of seeds, and at 400 variables it finished no dataset within
3,600 s. A target without causes keeps its distribution under these interventions, so its
experiment changes nothing; the I-contradicting test still marks the true arcs out of it, and
Andrey reverses 823 of those 3,960 arcs, the library 838, and GIES 16.

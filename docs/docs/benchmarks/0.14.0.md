::::{div} release-opening-text

This card measures the methods 0.14.0 makes supported against their authors' code: GnIES, UT-IGSP,
and IGSP on simulated data mixing observation and experiments, ICP on environments that leave one
variable's mechanism alone, CAM-UV on nonlinear data with hidden variables, and DYNOTEARS on vector
autoregressions. Each result holds for the measured conditions; it depends on the data, settings,
and hardware.

::::
:::::

0.14.0 makes six methods supported, and we compared each with the code its authors released, on
data simulated the way their papers do. GnIES, UT-IGSP, and IGSP learn from experiments mixed with
observation, ICP finds the causes of one variable across environments, CAM-UV handles nonlinear
data with hidden variables, and DYNOTEARS learns from time series.

## Summary

| Method | Reference | Comparisons | Better | Tie | Worse | Speed against the reference |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GnIES | [gnies 0.3.3](https://pypi.org/project/gnies/0.3.3/) | 21 settings x 2 errors | 3 | 39 | 0 | 34x faster |
| UT-IGSP | [graphical-model-learning 0.1a8](https://pypi.org/project/graphical-model-learning/0.1a8/) | 56 settings x 2 errors | 1 | 111 | 0 | 1.7x slower |
| ICP | [InvariantCausalPrediction 0.8](https://cran.r-project.org/package=InvariantCausalPrediction) (R) | 3 settings x 2 tests | 0 | 6 | 0 | 41x to 760x faster |
| CAM-UV | [lingam 1.13.0](https://pypi.org/project/lingam/1.13.0/), [causal-learn 0.1.4.8](https://pypi.org/project/causal-learn/0.1.4.8/) | 9 settings x 3 references | 0 | 27 | 0 | 2.1x to 3.1x faster than lingam, 7.4x to 13.5x than causal-learn |
| DYNOTEARS | [CausalNex 0.12.2](https://pypi.org/project/causalnex/0.12.2/) | 38 settings | 5 | 32 | 1 | 1.1x to 3.4x faster in CPU time |
| IGSP | [graphical-model-learning 0.1a8](https://pypi.org/project/graphical-model-learning/0.1a8/) | 87 settings, 114 comparisons | 2 | 112 | 0 | 1.3x faster to 8.0x slower |

Andrey matches every reference in almost every comparison, and in the few that differ it is more
often better than worse. Here is how to read it:

- The speed is the median, over the datasets, of the reference's fit time divided by Andrey's on the
  same dataset, written as how many times faster or slower Andrey is.
- A comparison is better or worse when the 95% interval of the mean paired difference in errors lies
  entirely below or above 0.
- A better result has to hold twice. It counts only where a rerun on fresh seeds, which played no
  part in choosing Andrey's defaults, agreed. A comparison better on the first seeds alone counts as
  a tie, and so do 17 UT-IGSP comparisons that were lower on the first seeds and on the seeds that
  chose its default, since those seeds are not fresh.
- Andrey must also beat doing nothing wherever the reference does, making fewer errors than the
  empty estimate: the empty graph, or for a target error no targets beyond those given. Where the
  reference does not beat it either, the floor is reported and not judged. The floor is judged on
  the first seeds; a rerun's floor only confirms a failure there.

## How we measured

Every dataset was simulated with `andrey.data` and given to both packages, so each comparison pairs
Andrey's error with the reference's on the same data. D in the tables is the mean of those paired
differences, Andrey's error minus the reference's, with its 95% bootstrap interval.

- GnIES, UT-IGSP, and IGSP ran one fit at a time on one core of an AMD EPYC 7713 with one BLAS
  thread.
- ICP, CAM-UV, and DYNOTEARS ran four fits at a time, each on one core of an Intel Core i7-7820X
  with one thread.
- GnIES and UT-IGSP run each package at its defaults, and Andrey's defaults were chosen on seeds of
  their own before the comparison. The other references run at the comparison's settings, given in
  each method's section below.

## GnIES

GnIES learns a graph from environments that change the noise of some variables, without being told
which. Andrey is about 34 times faster than gnies 0.3.3 and as accurate: of the 8 comparisons
better on seeds 0 to 19, 3 were better again on seeds 20 to 39, and the rest tie.

- The graphs are Erdos-Renyi DAGs on 10 variables, with linear equations and Gaussian noise.
- One environment is observational; each of the others changes the noise variance of its targets,
  and neither package is told the targets.
- Each environment has 10 to 1,000 rows, standardized on the pooled rows, with 20 datasets per
  setting.
- Both packages run at their defaults.
- We count two errors: the structural Hamming distance (SHD) of the returned interventional
  essential graph to the true one, and the target error, the targets missed plus the targets named
  wrongly.

Over the 420 datasets in the claim, Andrey took a median 0.030 times the reference's fit time
(quartiles 0.027 and 0.033). At 10 variables Andrey's median fit took 0.29 to 1.33 s per setting,
the reference's 9.1 to 48.6 s.

## UT-IGSP

UT-IGSP learns from experiments whose targets it is told only in part, or not at all, and also
learns those targets. At Andrey's defaults it ties the authors' library in 111 of 112 comparisons
and is better in one: the SHD with perfect interventions and 1 off-target, lower on seeds 0 to 19
and again on fresh seeds 2000 to 2019. 17 more were lower on seeds 0 to 19 and on seeds 4000 to
4019, the seeds of the run that chose the default, which favor it, so they count as ties. Run at
Andrey's level, one over the observational rows, the library ties Andrey in 107 of the 112.

- The graphs are Erdos-Renyi DAGs on 20 variables with mean degree 1.5.
- An observational environment comes with 5 experiments. Each hits one designed target and 0 to 3
  off-targets the fit is not told, in six groups of settings built from mean shifts, perfect
  interventions, variance changes, and weight changes.
- Each experiment has 1,000 to 5,000 rows, with 20 datasets per setting and 5 draws each.
- Both packages run at their defaults.
- We count the SHD of the returned interventional essential graph to the true one, and the target
  error.

UT-IGSP is slower than the library. At 20 variables its median fit took 0.24 s against the
library's 0.14 s, a median ratio of 1.68 per dataset (quartiles 1.47 and 1.93). At 50 variables it
took 1.7 s against 0.9 s, and at 100 variables 15.2 s against 16.8 s. A later change in this release
makes UT-IGSP prepare the rows about 1.2 times faster on large data (one core, 20 variables, 5
single-target experiments); these comparisons were not rerun after it.

## IGSP

IGSP learns from experiments whose targets are known. At Andrey's defaults, on fresh seeds, it is
worse than the authors' library in none of 114 comparisons and lower in 17. Two of those count as
better: at 1,000 rows the default level is 1e-3, so seeds 0 to 19 at that level measured the same
comparison, and they agreed. With both packages at the same one of three fixed levels, no row of
684 is worse once rerun, and 13 are better.

- The graphs are the 7-variable DAG of Wang et al. (2017) and Erdos-Renyi DAGs on 10 or 20 variables
  with mean degree 1.5, with linear equations and Gaussian noise.
- An observational environment comes with settings whose targets are known, hit by perfect,
  inhibiting, or imperfect interventions, at the sizes of Wang et al. (2017) and Yang et al. (2018).
- Each setting has 1,000 to 100,000 rows, with 20 datasets per setting and 5 draws each.
- Andrey's default level is one over the observational rows, as UT-IGSP's. The library runs at its
  defaults but for the level.
- Where a setting has two or more targets, Andrey is also compared with a copy of the library whose
  I-contradicting test follows Definition 4.2 of Yang et al.; the library's own test inverts that
  definition's conditions.
- We count the SHD of the returned interventional essential graph to the true one.

IGSP is slower than the library in 84 of the 87 settings of its claim. Per setting its median fit
took 0.74 to 8.0 times as long: a median of 1.8 times at 1,000 rows per setting, 4.3 at 10,000, and
3.5 at 100,000, and at most about 2 s. These times were measured at alpha 1e-3 before the faster row
preparation in this release, and were not rerun after it. Out of claim, at 100 variables and 1,000
rows, the library finished 1 of 10 datasets within 3,600 s and used up to 72 GB of memory; Andrey
took a median 33 s and at most 0.5 GB.

## ICP

ICP returns the causal parents of one target, so both packages fit the same datasets and we compare
the fits one by one. Pooled over each setting's sizes, every comparison ties, and Andrey is 41 to
760 times faster.

- The data follow linear Gaussian equations on 5 to 40 variables, as in the ICP paper's Appendix G,
  in two environments of 100 to 500 rows.
- A second design applies do-interventions on 6 or 10 variables in 3 or 4 environments of 200 rows.
- Both packages run at alpha 0.05 and test every set of the other variables on every row.
- We count the target's parents missed plus the variables named wrongly as parents.

With two environments the approximate test accepted the same sets as R on all 5,220 paired fits.
Every other difference, 2,112 fits, traces to one of two choices, each rebuilt from the rows. With
three or more environments Andrey corrects the approximate test by the number of environments, `K`,
as the paper does, where R uses `K - 1`; and R's exact test uses a larger residual variance.

Split by size, two rows differ. One approximate-test row is worse: with do-interventions on 6
variables, the `K` correction gives Andrey 0.024 more errors per fit (interval +0.002 to +0.046),
all from parents it does not name. Andrey follows the paper's `K` there, so the row is allowlisted
as correct by the paper. One exact-test row is lower: on 9 variables with two environments, Andrey
makes 0.012 fewer errors per fit (interval -0.022 to -0.003), on one set of seeds and not rerun.

```{raw} html
:file: 0.14.0/icp.html
```

ICP's guarantee holds in the claim, where the environments leave the target's own mechanism alone:
the FWER stays well below alpha in every setting, at most 0.0123, and no scenario's FWER is above
alpha beyond sampling error. Where an intervention can hit the target, a set holding a child of the
target stays nearly invariant and is accepted, and the FWER rises to 0.18 and 0.11; R gives the same
on the same fits (0.1936 and 0.1227). Given the targets, `targets` leaves the environments that hit
the target out of the fit.

With the approximate test at 9 to 15 variables and 1,000 rows, Andrey's median fit took 0.010 to
0.24 s against R's 0.72 to 58 s, 72 to 241 times faster. With the exact test, which reads the same
matrices in Andrey and solves a system per environment and set in R, it was 458 to 1,554 times
faster. Over the paired fits the median ratio ran from 41 to 760.

## CAM-UV

CAM-UV learns nonlinear graphs where some variables are hidden. Every setting ties each reference
once rerun, lingam at its default set size included. The two settings of the paper's simulation
were better on seeds 0 to 19 and tied on seeds 20 to 39.

- The data have nonlinear additive effects on 4 to 50 observed variables, in the paper's simulation,
  with one node in five or in ten hidden, or with none hidden.
- Each dataset has 500 to 2,000 rows, with 20 datasets per setting.
- The references search sets of up to 3 variables, the paper's size and Andrey's default; lingam
  also runs at its default of 2.
- We count the directed-edge errors against the edges CAM-UV can identify as direct, 2 for a
  reversed edge.

With one hidden variable in five, no implementation beats the empty graph. At 8 observed and 2
hidden variables, Andrey made 1.50 more errors per graph than the empty graph on seeds 0 to 19
(interval +0.30 to +2.75) and 1.20 more on seeds 20 to 40 (-0.20 to +2.60); lingam and causal-learn
made 1.85 to 1.90 more and 1.20 to 1.30 more. The empty graph is a floor only for a setting where
the reference's own interval against it lies below 0, so these settings are judged against the
references alone.

Andrey's graph differs from lingam's on 217 datasets. We traced 202 of them, every one below 50
observed variables, to five documented choices: the visiting order, the HSIC test, the regressions,
phase 2, and the cycle drop. The other 15, at 50 observed variables, are not traced.

CAM-UV ran 2.1 to 3.1 times faster than lingam and 7.4 to 13.5 times faster than causal-learn, each
searching sets of up to 3 variables, and 1.9 to 3.2 times faster than lingam at its default set size
of 2. At 50 observed variables its median fit took 91 s against lingam's 296 s and causal-learn's
964 s.

## DYNOTEARS

DYNOTEARS learns the links within and across time steps of a time series. At 500 steps every
setting whose noise has mean 0 ties CausalNex, with an F1 of at least 0.968 for both packages. With
exponential noise of mean 1, as the paper's simulations draw it, CausalNex cannot fit the series'
mean, and Andrey made fewer errors at every size at 500 steps and at 5 variables at 50 steps. On
seeds 20 to 39, five of those six were better again; at 100 variables and 500 steps the rerun's
interval reaches 0, so that setting counts as a tie.

- The series are linear vector autoregressions with one lag, from the DYNOTEARS paper, on
  Erdos-Renyi or Barabasi-Albert graphs with Gaussian or exponential noise.
- They have 5 to 100 variables and 50 or 500 steps, with 20 series per setting.
- Both packages run at the paper's settings for each length. CausalNex fits without a threshold and
  then keeps the weights above the paper's thresholds.
- We count the SHD over the same-step and the lagged edges.

One setting is worse: exponential noise of mean 0 at 100 variables and 50 steps, by 3.20 errors per
series on seeds 0 to 19 and 4.15 on seeds 20 to 39 (interval +1.30 to +7.10). It comes from
centering. Andrey fits each column around its own mean, the same as an intercept, which the paper's
loss leaves out. Refitted without centering, Andrey ties CausalNex there once rerun. Centering costs
errors on short series whose noise has mean 0, and removes 2.3 to 11.1 errors per series at 500
steps when the noise has mean 1.

DYNOTEARS used 1.1 to 3.4 times less CPU time than CausalNex, per setting the median ratio over the
paired series, on one core of an Intel Core i7-7820X. At 100 variables and 500 steps its median fit
took 299 s against CausalNex's 393 s.

## Other changes in 0.14.0

The [changelog](../changelog.md) measures the other changes: the interventional simulator
(`SCM.do`, `hard`, `soft`, and `envs=`), `env` and `targets` on every method that learns from
environments (`gies`'s `interventions` and `cdnod`'s `domain_index` still work in this release, with
a deprecation warning), the tie rank that ignores offsets, and the faster categorical tests.

## Limits

Each result holds for the settings above. These are the places where the methods, or both packages,
fall short:

- GnIES was measured on 10 variables, linear-Gaussian data, and environments that change only the
  noise variance of their targets. With 10 rows per environment neither package beats the empty
  graph in every setting: in B10 neither does, and in D10 Andrey does not on either seed set while
  the reference does on the rerun seeds only, so the floor, judged on the first seeds, does not
  apply there. Hard interventions, mean shifts, weight changes, non-Gaussian noise, nonlinear
  effects, and 20 to 100 variables lie outside the claim; the comparison page reports them without
  judging.
- UT-IGSP was measured on 20 variables and 5 experiments of 1,000 to 5,000 rows. At 50 variables
  with mean degree 5 and 3 experiments, neither package beats the empty graph. With 100 rows per
  experiment and no off-targets, Andrey names false targets more often than the library.
- IGSP was measured on linear Gaussian data with 7 to 20 variables, known targets, and 1,000 to
  100,000 rows per setting. Out of claim, with 100 rows per setting on 10 variables, Andrey made
  0.60 more errors than the library on one set of seeds, and at 400 variables it finished no dataset
  within 3,600 s. A target without causes keeps its distribution under these interventions, so its
  experiment changes nothing. The I-contradicting test still marks the true arcs out of it, and
  Andrey reverses 823 of those 3,960 arcs, the library 838, and GIES 16.
- ICP was measured on linear Gaussian data with 5 to 40 variables and 2 to 4 environments of 100 to
  500 rows. Its guarantee needs environments that leave the target's mechanism alone; where they do
  not, the FWER reaches 0.18, in R too, so pass the known targets. A hidden parent of the target
  raised the FWER to 0.018, and 0.145 in one scenario of 20. With at most 3 parents searched on 40
  variables, it found almost none of them.
- CAM-UV was measured on 4 to 50 observed variables and 500 to 2,000 rows. With one variable in five
  hidden, no package makes fewer errors than the empty graph: at 8 observed and 2 hidden variables
  Andrey makes 1.50 more on seeds 0 to 19 (interval +0.30 to +2.75) and 1.20 more on seeds 20 to 40
  (interval -0.20 to +2.60), and lingam and causal-learn 1.85 to 1.90 and 1.20 to 1.30 more. With
  nothing hidden every marked pair is false, and each package marks 3.2 to 3.6 pairs per graph.
- DYNOTEARS was measured on linear vector autoregressions with one lag, 5 to 100 variables, and 50
  or 500 steps, at the paper's settings for each length. With noise of mean 0, centering costs
  errors on short series: at 100 variables and 50 steps, about 3 per series more than CausalNex. At
  its defaults on 50 steps, both packages make more errors than the empty window from 20 variables
  on.

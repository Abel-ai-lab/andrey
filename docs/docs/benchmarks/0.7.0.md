::::{div} release-opening-text

We compare GES and the seven methods 0.7.0 supports, GIES, exact search, CD-NOD,
MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM, GFCI, GIN, and CALM, with the packages that implement them: causal-learn and
lingam in Python, pcalg in R, and Tetrad in Java. GES runs more than 60x faster than causal-learn at
50 to 150 variables, and more than 300x at 150, with the same graph on all 15 datasets. GIN runs
more than 70x faster than Tetrad and more than 2x faster than causal-learn at every size. CD-NOD
runs more than 15x faster than causal-learn at 20 and 50 variables, and CALM more than 15x faster
with its default schedule, both with fewer errors on more datasets. GFCI runs faster than Tetrad
from 50 variables, more than 4x at 200, with fewer errors at every size.

Speed is not accuracy. On the largest graphs, the errors of GES and GIES approach the empty graph's
in both packages (see [Limits](#limits)). Every dataset lists its variables in a random order, so
the column order tells no method anything about the causal order (see [Method](#method)). You can
find speedups and accuracy in the [summary](#summary). These results apply to the measured
workloads. Speed and accuracy depend on the data, settings, and hardware.

## Method

**Data.** Each method runs on data of the kind it is for. GES, exact search, and CALM run on
linear-Gaussian data from an Erdos-Renyi DAG with mean degree 2, standardized, with `n = 10 d`
rows. GIES adds interventions, simulated as in Hauser and Bühlmann (2012): `round(0.4 d)` variables
are each set by an intervention in their own block of rows, and every fit gets the rows'
intervention targets. CD-NOD pools 2,000 rows from four domains, in which one variable in three
shifts its mean. MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM gets three groups of 1,000 rows over the same DAG, linear
with uniform noise and weights of their own sign in each group. GFCI's data hide one node in ten,
and `d` counts the hidden nodes. GIN's data have `m` latent variables, each a cause of every later
one and each measured by three indicators, with 1,000 rows of the `3 m` indicators. There are 5
datasets per size, one per seed, and 20 for CD-NOD.

**Shuffled columns.** Every dataset lists its variables in an order drawn from its seed, with the
true graph permuted the same way, so the column order carries nothing of the causal order. GIN's
data list the indicators in a random order of their own.

**Accuracy.** Each method's errors count what it returns against the truth it can identify: the
SHD between CPDAGs for GES, exact search, and CALM; between interventional essential graphs for
GIES; between the CPDAG that the domain index orients for CD-NOD; and summed over the groups' DAGs
for MultiGroupDirectLiNGAM. The SHD counts one for each pair of variables whose edge is missing,
extra, or oriented differently, so the empty graph's errors are the number of true edges. GFCI's
errors are the endpoints whose mark differs from the true PAG's, so the empty graph's are twice the
true edges. GIN's error count is the latents whose indicators it does not return as one cluster;
the empty graph misses every latent. Errors are medians over a size's datasets. "Same graph" means
the two packages return the same result.

**Timing.** Times are seconds per fit, the median over a size's datasets. For each dataset, we
divide the other package's time by Andrey's, then report the median of these paired ratios. This
need not equal the ratio of the displayed median times. We show no speedup for a size at which both
packages' median times are under 0.1 s, and leave it out of the summary. Tetrad's time covers the
search inside the Java virtual machine, excluding startup and data reading. pcalg's time includes
converting the rows' intervention targets into its own. Andrey's time excludes Python's startup and
import. Times and ratios have three significant figures.

**Hardware.** Every fit ran on an AMD EPYC 7713 with 4 cores per fit and 4 BLAS and OpenMP
threads; a parallel mode runs 4 single-threaded worker processes. Fits ran one at a time on an
exclusive node, with 1 timed fit per dataset and a 30,000 MB memory cap. Every method but GIN and
CALM had 1 untimed warm-up fit. The wall cap was 1,800 s, and 3,600 s for CALM.

## GES

GES searches over equivalence classes, adding, then removing, the edges that most improve the score.
Both packages use the BIC with a penalty of `log(n)` per parameter and at most 4 parents, and
return a CPDAG; causal-learn halves the penalty's scale, so its `lambda_value=0.5` is Andrey's 1.
Andrey runs its NumPy backend in one process.

## Supported methods

### GIES

GIES learns from data that mix observation with interventions. Both packages optimize the same
BIC (pcalg's `GaussL0penIntScore` with its default penalty), run the forward, backward, and turning
phases until none improves, with no degree cap, and return the interventional essential graph.

### CD-NOD

CD-NOD learns from data pooled across domains, with the domain index as one more variable that
orients the edges it reaches. Both packages test with Fisher-Z at `alpha` 0.05 with a stable
skeleton search. Andrey orients conflicting colliders by the p-values that separated their ends
(`collider_order="p_value"`). causal-learn puts a collider wherever the middle node is in no
separating set, in the order it visits the triples.

| 10 | 2,000 | 0.00607 | 0.0470 | - | 20 of 20 | 16 of 20 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 10 | 3 | 1 |
| 20 | 2,000 | 0.0164 | 0.263 | 16.3x | 20 of 20 | 5 of 20 | 5 | 5.5 | 20 | 9 | 5 |

## Limits

- **Data coverage.** We measure one simulated regime per method, with mean degree 2, up to 200
  variables. These results do not establish performance on measured data or on other mechanisms.
- **GES at 200 variables.** Both packages' graphs have about as many errors as the empty graph (199
  against 200), so that row's speedup appears in its table only.
- **GIES on large graphs.** The errors grow toward the empty graph's with size: at 200 variables,
  they average 0.89 of the empty graph's, and one dataset of five has more errors than the empty
  graph, in both packages. pcalg returns the same graphs.
- **GFCI's parallel mode.** Running its GES step in four worker processes was slower than one
  process at every size measured (0.783-0.922x).
- **CALM's time.** At 50 variables, a fit takes about a minute with Andrey's default schedule and
  about 8 minutes with the paper's.
- **CD-NOD against causal-learn.** The two orient conflicting colliders by different rules, so they
  return the same graph on 16 of 20 datasets at 10 variables and on none at 50.
- **GIN's latent order.** With 3 and 4 latents, Andrey's result differs from both references' on 8
  of 10 datasets, in the order of the latents on every one. The rules differ: Andrey's GIN runs each
  root test once per cluster member and takes the root candidate whose lowest pooled p-value is
  highest, and causal-learn's and Tetrad's take the first candidate in list order that passes.
  Andrey's median order accuracy is 1 at 3 latents, against 0.667 for both references, and 1 at 4,
  against Tetrad's 0.667 and causal-learn's 0.333, but on 1 dataset Andrey leaves every latent
  unordered where both references order them.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured hardware, core budget, and settings.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. pcalg runs in the harness's R environment, and Tetrad through the driver described
in the guide. To rerun the comparisons, install Andrey 0.7.0 (andrey-core), causal-learn 0.1.4.8,
lingam 1.13.0, pcalg 2.7.12, causal-cmd 1.12.0, and tetrad-lib 7.6.10, then run the drivers
below. GIN's causal-learn side takes the column-index patch the harness applies
(`benchmarks/patch_causal_learn.sh`), and lingam runs in its own environment
(`benchmarks/start_lingam.sh`). Every driver shuffles the columns by default and runs each fit on 4
cores with a 30,000 MB memory cap. Times will differ with the hardware and build; graphs should
match closely.

| Comparison | Driver | d | n | Datasets x repeats | Wall cap (s) | Warm-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| GES against causal-learn | `ges_cpu.py` | 50, 100, 150, 200 | 10 per variable | 5 x 1 | 1,800 | 1 |
| GIES against pcalg | `gies_cpu.py` | 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 | 10 per variable | 5 x 1 | 1,800 | 1 |
| Exact search against causal-learn | `exact_cpu.py` | 5, 10, 15 | 10 per variable | 5 x 1 | 1,800 | 1 |
| CD-NOD against causal-learn | `cdnod_cpu.py` | 10, 20, 50 | 2,000 | 20 x 1 | 1,800 | 1 |

    --n 2000 --warmup 1 --cap-wall-s 1800 $common

    --seeds 5 --warmup 1 --cap-wall-s 1800 $common

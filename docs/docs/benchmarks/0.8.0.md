::::{div} release-opening-text

We compare PC and FCI with the four nonlinear conditional-independence tests 0.8.0 adds, KCI, RCoT,
CMIknn, and GPDC, with the packages that implement them: causal-learn in Python, pcalg with RCIT in
R, and tigramite in Python. We also compare BOSS and GRaSP with the BDeu score with causal-learn on
categorical data, and PC and FCI with the batched chi-square and G-square tests with Andrey 0.7.0.
PC with RCoT runs more than 10x faster than causal-learn at 50 variables, PC with KCI more than 2.5x
at every size we timed, and BOSS and GRaSP with BDeu more than 150x faster than causal-learn on
categorical data.

Speed is not accuracy. On nonmonotone data, Fisher-Z's errors pass the empty graph's from 10
variables, and the nonlinear tests stay below it in every package. Each test has no more errors than
its reference package on 75% to 98% of the datasets; see [Limits](#limits) for the sizes where the
reference is ahead. Every dataset lists its variables in a random order (see [Method](#method)). You
can find speedups and accuracy in the [summary](#summary). These results apply to the measured
workloads. Speed and accuracy depend on the data, settings, and hardware.

## Method

**Data.** The nonlinear tests run on two regimes over the same Erdos-Renyi DAGs with mean degree 2,
standardized, with Gaussian noise: additive data, `x = sum w (p + sin p) + e`, whose near-monotone
effects Fisher-Z still sees, and nonmonotone data, `x = sum w cos(p) + e`, whose even effects
partial correlation does not see. There are 20 datasets per size, one per seed, for accuracy; speed
is timed on the first 5. BOSS and GRaSP run on categorical data from an Erdos-Renyi DAG with mean
degree 2, 2 or 3 categories per variable, and Dirichlet(1) conditional tables, with 2,000 rows, and
on the diabetes network (413 variables) with 5,000 rows from its own tables. The categorical tests
run on the same random categorical graphs and on the alarm, hepar2, and andes networks with 1,000
and 5,000 rows. Those lanes have 5 datasets per size; the networks give one dataset per size.

**Shuffled columns.** Every dataset lists its variables in an order drawn from its seed, with the
true graph permuted the same way. The network timing rows against Andrey 0.7.0 keep the network's
column order; both builds see the same data.

**Accuracy.** PC's errors are the SHD between CPDAGs: one for each pair of variables whose edge is
missing, extra, or oriented differently, so the empty graph's errors are the number of true edges.
FCI's errors are the SHD against the true PAG of the observed variables: one for each pair whose
edge is missing, extra, or marked differently. Each nonlinear table also gives Andrey's PC or FCI
with Fisher-Z on the same datasets. Errors are medians over a size's datasets. "Same graph" means
the two packages return the same result. "Andrey minus" a package is the mean over the datasets
both finished of Andrey's errors minus the package's, with a 95% percentile bootstrap interval
(10,000 resamples); below 0, Andrey has fewer errors.

**Timing.** For the nonlinear tests, each timed dataset runs in 3 rounds on the same 4 cores: in
each round, Andrey and then the other package fit every dataset, each fit in its own process after
the row's warm-up fit (none where a fit can pass 300 s). A dataset's time is its median over the
rounds; for each dataset, we divide the other package's time by Andrey's, and the speedup is the
median of these ratios. A size gets a speedup only when both packages finished every timed dataset
in all 3 rounds on one CPU type; otherwise its table says why. In the other lanes, times are seconds
per fit, the median over a size's datasets, and the speedup is the median of the paired ratios. A
fit past its cap is coverage, never scored: the tables count the datasets both packages finished,
with a bound that counts each unfinished fit at the cap. We show no speedup for a size at which both
packages' median times are under 0.1 s. Times and ratios have three significant figures.

**Hardware.** Each lane names its machines in the summary's `hardware`:

- Nonlinear tests, accuracy: server nodes shared with other jobs, almost all AMD EPYC 7713, each fit
  on its own 4 cores with 4 BLAS and OpenMP threads and a 30,000 MB memory cap. Graphs do not depend
  on the CPU; which fits pass the cap can. The wall cap was 1,800 s, and 3,600 s for CMIknn, for KCI
  at 5,000 rows, and for GPDC at 2,000 rows.
- Nonlinear tests, speed: each row names its CPU, an AMD EPYC 7713, 9534, or 9555, or an Intel Xeon
  Gold 5418Y or 6542Y, with each fit on 4 pinned cores, most of them on a node shared with other
  jobs, and the same caps. A second machine, an Intel Core i7-7820X, timed some sizes on 2 physical
  cores and their 2 hyperthreads per fit, 3 sizes at once; its rows are listed apart and never
  compared with the servers'. Inside PC's skeleton, Andrey runs RCoT, CMIknn, and GPDC on one
  thread, and KCI on one thread below 2,000 rows.
- BOSS and GRaSP on categorical data: an Intel Core i7-7820X, each fit on 4 pinned cores, with
  nothing else running, 1 timed fit per dataset after 1 warm-up fit, a 3,600 s wall cap, and
  7,200 s with no warm-up for diabetes.
- Categorical tests against Andrey 0.7.0: an AMD EPYC 9534 exclusive node with every core visible
  to the fit and no core budget, each build in its own environment. The random-graph rows take 1
  timed fit after 1 warm-up; the network rows take the faster of 2 fits.
- FCI's feasible sizes: an AMD EPYC 7713, each fit on its own 4 cores, one fit at a time per core
  group on an exclusive node, and a 30,000 MB memory cap.

## Nonlinear tests

KCI, RCoT, CMIknn, and GPDC are settings of PC and FCI (`indep_test`). Both packages run the search
at `alpha` 0.05. The references differ by design: causal-learn's KCI drops null eigenvalues below
1e-5, which Andrey keeps; causal-learn and RCIT draw RCoT's random features from their own
generators; tigramite's CMIknn reports `b / B` where Andrey reports `(b + 1) / (B + 1)`; and
tigramite's GPDC uses its own Gaussian process and an analytic null, where Andrey draws a
rank-permutation null of 1,000 draws inside every fit. Each section gives accuracy on 20 datasets
per size, then speed on 5 of them, one row per size and CPU.

## Other changes in 0.8.0

The benchmark above covers the release's theme; 0.8.0 also changes the following. The
[changelog](../changelog.md) lists every change.

- **Background knowledge for every search.** PC, CD-NOD, FCI, GFCI, GES, GIES, XGES, hill climbing,
  tabu search, BOSS, GRaSP, exact search, and DirectLiNGAM take `knowledge=`: the edges the graph
  must have and must not have, the only pairs that may be adjacent, and tiers in time order. PC,
  FCI, GFCI, and CD-NOD also take `max_depth`.
- **Tie breaks and column order.** `andrey.config(tie_break=...)` chooses how the searches break
  ties: by a rank computed from each column's own data, the default, or by column position. The
  [column order guide](../guides/column-order.md) lists what each method keeps under a new column
  order and on another machine.
- **Score defaults that follow the data (breaking).** BOSS, GRaSP, GES, hill climbing, tabu search,
  and exact search pick BDeu for categorical data and the conditional Gaussian BIC for mixed data,
  and warn once; pass the linear-Gaussian BIC to keep the old default.
- **Faster categorical tests.** The chi-square and G-square tests answer many queries at once: on
  alarm, hepar2, and andes, PC returns the same graphs 4.30x to 6.42x faster than 0.7.0, and FCI
  1.99x to 4.70x (see [the chi-square and G-square tests](#the-chi-square-and-g-square-tests)).
- **BDeu and the categorical BIC for BOSS and GRaSP.** `score_func="local_score_BDeu"` and
  `"local_score_BIC_categorical"`, the scores the other score-based searches take for categorical
  data.
- **BOSS and GRaSP decide every comparison as full scores would**, in less time.
- **Clearer errors.** Copied and constant columns are named before any test runs, the LiNGAM methods
  say how many rows their pruning step needs, and a PDAG without a consistent extension names a
  chordless cycle.
- **Order-free ties across machines.** DirectLiNGAM, ICA-LiNGAM, exact search, and the score-based
  searches break ties by a rank computed from each column's own data, so they return the same graph
  on every CPU and under any column order; VARMA-LiNGAM and LongitudinalLiNGAM fit on rank-ordered
  columns.
- **FCI's measured limits.** FCI's and GFCI's documentation gives the sizes they finish within a cap
  (see [FCI's feasible sizes](#fcis-feasible-sizes)).
- **Column names.** Labels are text and unique in every method, the CLI, and graph input and output.

## Limits

- **Pending measurements.** See [Pending measurements](#pending-measurements).
- **KCI.** Measured up to 20 variables at 2,000 rows and 5 variables at 5,000 rows. causal-learn
  passed the 3,600 s cap on 8 of 20 additive and 5 of 20 nonmonotone datasets at 5 variables and
  5,000 rows; at 20 variables and 2,000 rows, on nonmonotone data, Andrey passed the 1,800 s cap on
  1 of 20 datasets and causal-learn on 6.
- **RCoT.** Every fit finished at every size up to 50 variables and 5,000 rows. causal-learn has
  fewer errors than Andrey on 4 of 20 datasets, and Andrey on none, with PC on additive data at 10
  variables and 5,000 rows and with FCI on additive data at 5 variables and 500 rows. pcalg has
  accuracy rows but no speed: it did not run in the timing rounds.
- **CMIknn.** Measured up to 20 variables at 500 rows, 10 at 1,000, and 5 at 2,000. A fit takes
  minutes at 10 variables and 500 rows (192 to 295 s on an AMD EPYC 7713). The speedup over
  tigramite depends on the CPU: 2.40x to 10.3x on the servers, 27.0x to 37.7x on the second
  machine.
- **GPDC.** Measured up to 20 variables at 1,000 rows and 5 at 2,000. The speedup over tigramite is
  1.38x to 1.89x on the servers and 1.20x to 1.46x on the second machine. At 20 variables and 1,000
  rows, tigramite passed the 1,800 s cap on 10 of 20 additive and 2 of 20 nonmonotone datasets, and
  Andrey on none.
- **FCI.** Measured with KCI and RCoT at 5 and 10 variables, with 500 and 1,000 rows.
- **Sizes timed on two CPU types.** A few sizes ran their rounds on two CPU types; their tables give
  no speedup until they are rerun on one.
- **BOSS and GRaSP on small graphs.** causal-learn's search has fewer errors on 1 or 2 of 5
  datasets at 10 and 20 variables, and for GRaSP at 40; the two return the same graph on most
  datasets up to 20 variables.
- **Categorical-test hardware.** The rows against Andrey 0.7.0 ran with every core of the node
  visible to the fit, unlike the other lanes' 4-core budget.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured CPU, core budget, and settings; the same size
  can give different ratios on two CPUs.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. To rerun the comparisons, install Andrey 0.8.0 (andrey-core), causal-learn
0.1.4.8, tigramite 5.2.10.1 in its own environment, and pcalg 2.7.12 with RCIT 0.1.0 in the
harness's R environment. Every driver shuffles the columns by default. Times will differ with the
hardware and build; graphs should match closely.

|---|---|---|---|---:|

| PC with the categorical tests | `discrete_cpu.py` | d 10, 20, 40 | 5 x 1 | 1,800 |

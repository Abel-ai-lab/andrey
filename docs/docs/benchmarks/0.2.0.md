# 0.2.0 release card: hidden common causes

This card measures two methods for data with hidden common causes, RCD and BottomUpParceLiNGAM,
compared with lingam, which implements both. Both return the same graph as lingam on 25 of 25
datasets and are 1.7-10.8x faster, so both are supported from 0.2.0. The same graph is not always
an accurate one: from `d = 10`, BottomUpParceLiNGAM's directed edges have more errors than the
empty graph's, in both packages (see [Known limits](#known-limits)). The [summary](#summary) gives
the speedups and the accuracy; [how to read the results](#how-to-read-the-results) says what was
measured and how it was scored. These results describe the measured workloads; speed and accuracy
depend on the data, settings, and hardware.

## Summary

lingam is the baseline. Speed and accuracy are reported separately: every paired speed ratio is
given, whatever the accuracy.

**Speed.**

| Family | Method | Compared with | Variables (`d`) | Andrey speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden common causes | RCD | lingam | 5-30 | 1.7-2.9x |
| Hidden common causes | BottomUpParceLiNGAM | lingam | 5-30 | 2.5-10.8x |

**Accuracy.**

| Method | Compared with | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| RCD | lingam | Same graph on 25 of 25 datasets |
| BottomUpParceLiNGAM | lingam | Same graph on 25 of 25 datasets |

## How to read the results

**Data.** Both methods use `latent_lingam_er`: Erdos-Renyi DAGs with mean degree 2, linear
mechanisms, uniform noise, standardized data, and 2,000 samples. One node in five is hidden, at
least one, and has no column, so it acts as a hidden common cause of its children. Here `d` counts
every node, hidden ones included: `d = 5, 10, 15, 20, 30` give 4, 8, 12, 16, and 24 observed
variables. There are five seeds per size.

**Accuracy.** "Same graph" means equal `structure_hash` values: the two packages return the same
edges with the same marks. Each estimate is also scored against the generated ground truth, on
the two things these methods estimate:

- **Directed edges**, against the latent projection: observed `i -> j` when a directed path runs
  from `i` to `j` through hidden nodes only, a direct edge included, less the pairs that share a
  hidden cause. The error count is the number of edges in one set and not the other, so a reversed
  edge counts twice; lower is better. The empty graph's count is the number of true edges.
- **Confounded pairs**, against the observed pairs that one hidden node reaches through hidden
  nodes only. RCD marks such a pair `<->`. BottomUpParceLiNGAM leaves a block of variables
  unordered, joined by `o-o`: the confounded variables, their observed ancestors, and any other
  confounded pairs, so its pairs include some with no shared cause. RCD is scored by F1 and
  BottomUpParceLiNGAM by recall, the share of true pairs it leaves unordered; higher is better.

The observed PAG, the target of FCI in the [0.1.0 release card](0.1.0.md), does not rank these
methods: they use their functional assumptions to mark `<->` where the PAG has `o-o`, so PAG scores
would count a correct `<->` as an error. Compare each method across the two packages, not RCD with
BottomUpParceLiNGAM.

**Timing.** Times are seconds per fit. Each fit runs in its own process after imports, with no
warm-up fit and one fit per dataset. Wall caps include setup and the timed fit; memory caps cover
the process tree. Each speed ratio divides lingam's time by Andrey's on the same dataset, then
takes the median over datasets, so ratios need not equal ratios of the displayed median times.

**Hardware.** Every fit used 2 cores of an AMD EPYC 7713 on a shared node, so times include
possible contention. Ratios apply within this campaign and do not transfer between machines.

## Hidden-confounder methods

Both packages run each method with its defaults: RCD regresses out at most 2 variables at a time,
with its correlation, independence, and normality tests at a significance level of 0.01, and
BottomUpParceLiNGAM accepts a sink at a significance level of 0.1, Bonferroni-corrected.

### RCD

RCD regresses variables on candidate ancestors and tests the residuals for independence to find
each variable's ancestors, then its parents, and then the pairs that share a hidden common cause.

| d | Observed variables | Andrey | lingam | Speedup |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 4 | 1.99 | 3.31 | 1.66x |
| 10 | 8 | 3.75 | 8.81 | 2.35x |
| 15 | 12 | 6.83 | 16.7 | 2.17x |
| 20 | 16 | 11.9 | 34.7 | 2.88x |
| 30 | 24 | 17.9 | 52.5 | 2.55x |

| d | Same graph | Directed errors, Andrey | Directed errors, lingam | Empty-graph directed errors | Pair F1, Andrey | Pair F1, lingam |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 0.657 | 0.657 |
| 10 | 5 of 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 0.344 | 0.344 |
| 15 | 5 of 5 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 0.454 | 0.454 |
| 20 | 5 of 5 | 8 | 8 | 11 | 0.543 | 0.543 |
| 30 | 5 of 5 | 11 | 11 | 19 | 0.467 | 0.467 |

RCD's directed errors are below the empty graph's at every size but `d = 10`, where they equal it.

### BottomUpParceLiNGAM

BottomUpParceLiNGAM finds sinks one at a time, from the last variable in the causal order up, and
leaves the variables a hidden common cause keeps it from ordering in one unordered block.

| d | Observed variables | Andrey | lingam | Speedup |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 4 | 1.84 | 4.59 | 2.48x |
| 10 | 8 | 8.66 | 46.9 | 5.08x |
| 15 | 12 | 21.7 | 126 | 5.68x |
| 20 | 16 | 39.9 | 279 | 6.61x |
| 30 | 24 | 95.9 | 1,030 | 10.8x |

| d | Same graph | Directed errors, Andrey | Directed errors, lingam | Empty-graph directed errors | Pair recall, Andrey | Pair recall, lingam |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 5 of 5 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 0.233 | 0.233 |
| 10 | 5 of 5 | 11 | 11 | 4 | 0.145 | 0.145 |
| 15 | 5 of 5 | 17 | 17 | 8 | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| 20 | 5 of 5 | 22 | 22 | 11 | 0 | 0 |
| 30 | 5 of 5 | 40 | 40 | 19 | 0 | 0 |

From `d = 10`, BottomUpParceLiNGAM's directed errors are higher than the empty graph's, and from
`d = 20` it leaves none of the confounded pairs unordered. Both packages return the same graphs, so
these are the method's results on this data, not a difference between the implementations.

### CAM-UV is not reported

The campaign's driver also runs CAM-UV, compared with lingam on `latent_anm_er`: the same graphs
and hidden nodes with nonlinear mechanisms and Gaussian additive noise. The two implementations
differ inside: Andrey regresses with spline features and least squares where lingam fits a
generalized additive model, and their independence tests use different kernel bandwidths and null
approximations, so the same settings give different graphs. CAM-UV stays experimental, and its
results are not part of this card.

## Known limits

- **Data coverage.** One regime: sparse linear Erdos-Renyi graphs with uniform noise, one node in
  five hidden, and 2,000 samples, up to `d = 30`. These results do not establish performance across
  all data regimes.
- **Scale.** RCD's and BottomUpParceLiNGAM's fit times grow quickly with the number of variables:
  from 4 to 24 observed variables, Andrey's median time grows from 1.99 to 17.9 s for RCD and from
  1.84 to 95.9 s for BottomUpParceLiNGAM, and lingam's from 3.31 to 52.5 s and from 4.59 to
  1,030 s.
- **Errors against the empty graph.** BottomUpParceLiNGAM's median directed-edge errors are higher
  than the empty graph's at `d = 10`, 15, 20, and 30 (11, 17, 22, and 40 against 4, 8, 11, and
  19). RCD's equal the empty graph's at `d = 10` (4 against 4). Andrey and lingam return the same
  graphs, so these results come from the methods on this data, not from either implementation.
- **BottomUpParceLiNGAM's block.** Every pair in its unordered block is joined by `o-o`, whether or
  not the pair shares a hidden cause, and edges inside the block stay unoriented, which caps its
  directed-edge recall.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured hardware, core budget, and settings.

## Reproduce

The [benchmark harness guide][harness] documents environment setup, the campaign driver, and the
scores. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey 0.2.0 (`andrey-core`) and lingam 1.12.2, and run
the driver with the settings below. Times will differ with the hardware and the build; graphs and
scores should match closely.

| Comparison | Driver | d | Seeds x repeats | Warm-up fits | Wall cap (s) | Cores |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RCD, BottomUpParceLiNGAM | `confounded_cpu.py` | 5-30 | 5 x 1 | 0 | 1,800 | 2 |

The wall cap covers setup and the timed fit, and memory was capped at 20,000 MB per fit. The
driver runs each size's datasets for every method it holds, CAM-UV included:

```shell
$ANDREY_BENCH_PYTHON benchmarks/confounded_cpu.py --out outputs/confounded-cpu \
    --ladder 5 10 15 20 30 --seeds 5 --n 2000 --cap-wall-s 1800 --cap-mem-mb 20000 --cores 2
```

# 0.2.0 release card: hidden common causes

We compare RCD and BottomUpParceLiNGAM with lingam, which implements both methods for data with
hidden common causes. Each returns the same graph as lingam on 25 of 25 datasets and runs
1.7-10.8x faster. We therefore support both from 0.2.0.

Graph agreement does not ensure accuracy. Here `d` counts all nodes, including hidden nodes.
From `d = 10`, BottomUpParceLiNGAM's directed edges have more errors than the empty graph's in
both packages (see [Known limits](#known-limits)). You can find speedups and accuracy in the
[summary](#summary), and measurement and scoring definitions in
[how to read the results](#how-to-read-the-results). These results apply to the measured workloads;
speed and accuracy depend on the data, settings, and hardware.

## Summary

lingam is the baseline. We report every paired speed ratio, regardless of accuracy.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden common causes | RCD | lingam | 5-30 | 1.7-2.9x |
| Hidden common causes | BottomUpParceLiNGAM | lingam | 5-30 | 2.5-10.8x |

**Accuracy.**

|---|---|---|

## How to read the results

**Data.** We evaluate both methods on `latent_lingam_er`: Erdos-Renyi directed acyclic graphs
(DAGs) with mean degree 2, linear mechanisms, uniform noise, standardized data, and 2,000 samples.
One node in five is hidden, with at least one hidden node. Hidden nodes have no data columns and
act as hidden common causes of their children. The sizes `d = 5, 10, 15, 20, 30` give 4, 8, 12,
16, and 24 observed variables. There are five seeds per size.

**Accuracy.** "Same graph" means equal `structure_hash` values: the packages return the same
edges with the same marks. We also score each estimate against the generated ground truth for
the two types of relationships these methods estimate:

- **Directed edges**, compared with the latent projection: an observed edge `i -> j` exists when
  a directed path runs from `i` to `j` through hidden nodes only, including a direct edge, but
  excluding pairs that share a hidden cause. The error count is the number of edges in one set
  but not the other. A reversed edge counts twice; lower is better. The empty graph's count is
  the number of true edges.
- **Confounded pairs**, compared with the observed pairs that a single hidden node reaches
  through hidden nodes only. RCD marks such a pair `<->`. BottomUpParceLiNGAM leaves a block of
  variables unordered, joined by `o-o`. This block includes the confounded variables, their
  observed ancestors, and any other confounded pairs, so some pairs have no shared cause.
  We score RCD by F1 and BottomUpParceLiNGAM by recall, the share of true pairs it leaves
  unordered. Higher is better.

The observed partial ancestral graph (PAG), the target of FCI in the
[0.1.0 release card](0.1.0.md), is unsuitable for ranking these methods. Their functional
assumptions let them mark `<->` where the PAG has `o-o`, so PAG scores would count a correct
`<->` as an error. Compare each method across the two packages, not RCD with BottomUpParceLiNGAM.

**Timing.** Times are seconds per fit. Each fit runs in its own process and is timed after
imports, with no warm-up fit and one fit per dataset. Wall caps include setup and the timed fit;
memory caps cover the process tree. For each dataset, we divide lingam's time by Andrey's, then
report the median of these paired ratios. This need not equal the ratio of the displayed median
times.

**Hardware.** Every fit used 2 cores of an AMD EPYC 7713 on a shared node, so times may include
contention. Ratios apply within this campaign and do not transfer between machines.

## Hidden-confounder methods

We use each method's defaults in both packages. RCD regresses out at most 2 variables at a time
and uses a significance level of 0.01 for its correlation, independence, and normality tests.
BottomUpParceLiNGAM accepts a sink at a Bonferroni-corrected significance level of 0.1.

### RCD

RCD regresses variables on candidate ancestors and tests the residuals for independence. It finds
each variable's ancestors, then its parents, then the pairs that share a hidden common cause.

| 5 | 4 | 1.99 | 3.31 | 1.66x |
| 10 | 8 | 3.75 | 8.81 | 2.35x |
| 15 | 12 | 6.83 | 16.7 | 2.17x |
| 20 | 16 | 11.9 | 34.7 | 2.88x |
| 30 | 24 | 17.9 | 52.5 | 2.55x |

| d | Same graph | Directed errors, Andrey | Directed errors, lingam | Empty-graph directed errors | Pair F1, Andrey | Pair F1, lingam |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

RCD's directed errors are below the empty graph's at every size except `d = 10`, where they
are equal.

### BottomUpParceLiNGAM

BottomUpParceLiNGAM finds sinks one at a time, working backward from the last variable in the
causal order. Variables it cannot order because of a hidden common cause remain in one unordered
block.

| d | Observed variables | Andrey | lingam | Speedup |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 4 | 1.84 | 4.59 | 2.48x |
| 10 | 8 | 8.66 | 46.9 | 5.08x |
| 15 | 12 | 21.7 | 126 | 5.68x |
| 20 | 16 | 39.9 | 279 | 6.61x |
| 30 | 24 | 95.9 | 1,030 | 10.8x |

| d | Same graph | Directed errors, Andrey | Directed errors, lingam | Empty-graph directed errors | Pair recall, Andrey | Pair recall, lingam |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

From `d = 10`, BottomUpParceLiNGAM's directed errors exceed the empty graph's. From `d = 20`,
it leaves none of the confounded pairs unordered.

### CAM-UV is not reported

The campaign driver also runs CAM-UV, compared with lingam on `latent_anm_er`: the same graphs
and hidden nodes, with nonlinear mechanisms and Gaussian additive noise. Andrey uses spline
features and least squares for regression; lingam fits a generalized additive model. Their
independence tests also use different kernel bandwidths and null approximations. These
differences produce different graphs under the same settings. CAM-UV remains experimental,
and we exclude its results from this card.

## Known limits

- **Data coverage.** We measure one regime: sparse linear Erdos-Renyi graphs with uniform noise,
  one node in five hidden, and 2,000 samples, up to `d = 30`. These results do not establish
  performance across all data regimes.
- **Scale.** Fit times for both methods grow quickly with the number of variables. From 4 to 24
  observed variables, Andrey's median time grows from 1.99 to 17.9 s for RCD and from 1.84 to
  95.9 s for BottomUpParceLiNGAM. In lingam, it grows from 3.31 to 52.5 s and from 4.59 to
  1,030 s.
- **Errors against the empty graph.** BottomUpParceLiNGAM's median directed-edge errors are higher
  than the empty graph's at `d = 10`, 15, 20, and 30 (11, 17, 22, and 40 compared with 4, 8, 11,
  and 19). RCD's equal the empty graph's at `d = 10` (4 compared with 4). Andrey and lingam return
  the same graphs, so these results reflect the methods on this data, not differences between
  implementations.
- **BottomUpParceLiNGAM's block.** Edges inside the unordered block remain unoriented, limiting
  directed-edge recall.
- **Hardware.** Ratios are specific to the measured hardware, core budget, and settings.

## Reproduce

The [benchmark harness guide][harness] documents environment setup, the campaign driver, and the
scores. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey 0.2.0 (andrey-core) and lingam 1.12.2, then run
the driver with the settings below. Times will differ with the hardware and build; graphs and
scores should match closely.

| Comparison | Driver | d | Seeds x repeats | Warm-up fits | Wall cap (s) | Cores |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RCD, BottomUpParceLiNGAM | `confounded_cpu.py` | 5-30 | 5 x 1 | 0 | 1,800 | 2 |

Memory was capped at 20,000 MB per fit. The driver runs each size's datasets for every method it
includes, CAM-UV included:

```shell
$ANDREY_BENCH_PYTHON benchmarks/confounded_cpu.py --out outputs/confounded-cpu \
    --ladder 5 10 15 20 30 --seeds 5 --n 2000 --cap-wall-s 1800 --cap-mem-mb 20000 --cores 2
```
